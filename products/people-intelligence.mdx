Corporate Registrations tells you the company is real. **People Intelligence** tells you who runs it.

It answers the questions diligence asks about a counterparty's leadership — who is accountable, how deep the bench is, whether anyone named is carrying a sanctions or enforcement problem — and it answers them from public sources, without contacting the vendor.

It appears as a subtab of a vendor's **Vendor Intelligence** section, alongside Corporate Registrations, Financial Health and Security Intelligence.

## Confirmed and unconfirmed are not the same thing

This is the distinction the whole page is built around, so it is worth understanding before anything else on it.

Researching people is dominated by one failure mode: **namesakes**. Search for the leadership of a mid-sized company and you will reliably surface a well-known executive at a similarly-named firm. Presenting that person as the vendor's CFO is worse than returning nothing, because it looks like a finding.

So every name is placed in one of two groups:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Confirmed" icon="circle-check">
    Tied to this vendor by a source that resolved through the vendor's own domain, or by a matched professional profile. These are the only names that are counted, charted, or scored.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Unconfirmed" icon="circle-question">
    Surfaced in research but not tied to this vendor with confidence. Shown so you can judge them yourself — and deliberately excluded from every count on the page.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Warning>
  Do not quote an unconfirmed name as the vendor's staff. It is a research lead, not a finding. The section is there so you can see what was considered and rejected, which is more useful than silently dropping it.
</Warning>

## What the page shows

**Coverage.** Four figures at the top: confirmed people, executives and board currently serving, how many of those are corroborated by a registry filing, and adverse findings. A person named as an officer in a public filing is a materially stronger claim than one scraped from a leadership page, which is why the two are counted separately.

**Adverse media and screening.** Negative news, sanctions matches and enforcement matters tied to the people above, each with a severity, a category and a link to the source.

**Reporting lines.** Where published reporting relationships could be established, they are drawn as a tree. This is partial by nature — it reflects what is public, not the vendor's real org chart.

**People.** A card per person: title, role, tenure, prior roles, and links to the profiles that back them. Former staff are labelled as such rather than dropped, because a recent executive departure is often the thing you actually wanted to know.

## "No findings" and "not screened" are different

If adverse-media screening could not run — a source was unreachable, or the vendor has not been screened yet — the page says **Not screened**. It does not show a zero.

<Warning>
  A zero would read as a clean record, and the two mean opposite things. Anywhere this data appears — the page, the Zero Touch score, the API, an agent answering through MCP — an unscreened vendor is reported as unknown, never as clear.
</Warning>

This also governs scoring: when screening has not run, the People component of a [Zero Touch Score](/user-guides/zero-touch-assessments) deducts nothing at all, and labels itself so nobody reads the resulting number as a clean bill of health.

## Where the data comes from

| Source | What it contributes |
| --- | --- |
| **Org chart data** | Serving staff resolved through the vendor's own domain, with titles, reporting lines and profile links |
| **Web research** | Leadership named on the vendor's own site and in public profiles |
| **Public registers** | Officers and directors named in corporate filings |
| **Sanctions lists** | OFAC SDN and consolidated list screening against the confirmed names |

A sanctions match is reported as a **high** severity finding, and always with the caveat that name similarity is not identity. Confirm against the listing itself before acting on one.

## Using it

- **Before an assessment**, to see whether there is an accountable leadership team at all. A vendor with no one on record is not disqualified, but it is a different conversation.
- **As a Zero Touch component**, so leadership visibility and adverse media feed the triage score directly. See [Zero Touch Assessments](/user-guides/zero-touch-assessments).
- **Through an agent**, via the Coverbase MCP server: `query_vendors(kind="leadership", vendor_id="cbvndr_...")`. Note this is distinct from `kind="people"`, which returns *your own team's* owners and watchers on that vendor.

## Limits worth knowing

- Coverage reflects what a company publishes. A private company that names nobody publicly will show little here, and that is a fact about its disclosure, not about its governance.
- Reporting lines are reconstructed from published relationships and are usually incomplete.
- People with the same name are kept as separate records rather than merged. A nickname and a legal first name will appear as two people — a visible duplicate is a better failure than a wrongly merged pair.
- Nothing here is a background check. It is open-source research, and it is a starting point for a conversation, not a conclusion about an individual.
