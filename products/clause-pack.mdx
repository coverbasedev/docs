Due diligence finds risk; the contract is where you hold the vendor to controlling it. A clause pack closes that loop. Your legal team maps clauses to risk domains once. For each engagement, Coverbase then lists the clauses its risk results and open issues require, shows whether the current draft carries each one, and, once the contract is signed, turns the obligations those clauses carry into activities on the engagement's monitoring plan.

## What it does

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Clauses required by risk domain" icon="link">
    On a reference clause in **Clause Sets**, **Required by Risk Domain** names the risk domain and minimum level that require it, optionally also whenever the domain has an open issue, and what to monitor after signing.
  </Card>
  <Card title="A pack per engagement" icon="list-check">
    The engagement's **Contract** tab lists each required clause, why it is required (for example **Privacy · High** or **Open issue**), and whether the draft has it: **Present**, **Missing**, **Non-standard**, **Added to redline** or **Not reviewed**.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Remediation covenants" icon="handshake">
    An open issue with a remediation due date adds a covenant to the pack, so the contract holds the vendor to the fix.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Obligations monitored after signature" icon="calendar-check">
    When the contract is signed, each required clause that made it into the contract and carries an obligation becomes an evidence request or scorecard activity on the monitoring plan, on its cadence. Event obligations are watched through Radar and issues instead.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Non-standard Contracts report" icon="table">
    Every clause whose latest review found it non-conforming, missing or needing review, across all contracts, filterable by severity, status and risk domain, and exportable as CSV.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## The rules it follows

- **The pack reads what the Risk Summary reads.** It uses the same domain results (residual where there is one) and the same open issues, so the pack cannot disagree with the decision it follows.
- **Clause review decides what the draft says.** The draft state comes from the latest complete [Contract Guardian](/products/contract-guardian) review of each contract linked to the engagement. The pack only marks a clause for the redline.
- **Rebuilding never drops a signed obligation.** A clause no longer required leaves the pack, unless it is already in the signed contract.
- **Only signed clauses are monitored.** At signature, a clause the draft held or that was added to the redline counts as signed. One still missing or non-standard does not, and its obligation is not monitored.
