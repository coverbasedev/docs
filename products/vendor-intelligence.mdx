Vendor Intelligence answers two questions about a company without asking the company anything: **is this corporation real and in good standing**, and **is it financially sound enough to depend on**. This page covers the first: the validated corporate identity every other answer is computed against. The second is the [Financial Health Score](/products/financial-health-score).

Both are assembled from registries of record and regulatory filings, and every figure on screen names the source it came from.

<Note>
  This is an optional feature currently in beta. Let your Coverbase representative know if you'd like it turned on for your organization. It pairs with the [Financial Health Score](/products/financial-health-score) and [Security Intelligence](/products/security-intelligence), which follow the same beta arrangement.
</Note>

## What it does

  <Card title="Corporate registration validation" icon="building-columns">

  </Card>

  <Card title="Coverbase Financial Health Score" icon="chart-line">

  </Card>

  <Card title="Corroboration over closeness" icon="scale-balanced">
    A short or ambiguous name binds only when two independent registers agree on the same legal entity. Agreement between registers outranks a few points of name similarity.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Citations on every fact" icon="link">

  </Card>

## Corporate registration

Coverbase searches each register by company name, scores every candidate against the vendor's name, and binds a record only on a high-confidence match. A short or ambiguous name (for example, "Meta") binds only when two independent registers agree on the same legal entity.

When nothing matches confidently, the card shows **Needs review** rather than quietly picking the closest hit. That distinction matters: binding the wrong "Acme Holdings" is worse than returning nothing.

| Source | What it establishes |
| ------ | ------------------- |
| **GLEIF** | Authoritative legal name, LEI, entity and registration status, jurisdiction, legal and headquarters addresses |
| **OpenCorporates** | Company number, jurisdiction, incorporation date, current status, link to the source registry |
| **SEC EDGAR** | US public-company validation: CIK, ticker, state of incorporation, SIC industry |
| **Dun & Bradstreet** | DUNS number, where your organization has its own D&B entitlement |

## Financial Health Score

The other half of the answer lives on its own page. The **Coverbase Financial Health Score** is a 0-100 view of whether a vendor is financially sound enough to depend on, computed from filed statements where they exist and from funding, headcount, and corporate-standing signals where they do not, always labelled with which.

It depends on the corporate identity established above: the score is computed against the legal entity the registers bound, which is why a missed match shows up as a thinner score rather than a wrong one.

## How evidence is sourced

A common assumption is that "web search" means handing a query to a search engine and reading the top results. That is not how Coverbase gathers evidence.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Registries of record are consulted first">
    For any question about a company's identity, GLEIF, OpenCorporates, and SEC EDGAR are queried before any web crawl. Those facts enter as authoritative evidence in the prioritized bucket, ahead of every web result.
  </Step>

  <Step title="The register wins a conflict">
    Where a registry and a web page disagree on a corporate fact, the model is instructed that the registry is correct. A register states a company's legal name and status as a matter of record; a search engine states whatever ranked well today.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Web results are graded before they can be cited">
    Every candidate is classified for relevance to the specific control being evaluated **and** for publisher accountability: authoritative, reputable, or unreliable. Anything below the control set's minimum credibility is discarded, not merely ranked lower.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A control set configured to require authoritative sources will not cite a blog post. It will report that it found nothing, which is the correct answer.

## Availability

Vendor Intelligence is an optional feature in beta, enabled per organization. **Contact your Coverbase representative to have it turned on.**

Corporate registration validation is populated for every organization on each Vendor Intelligence refresh. What requires enabling is the presentation of it (the Corporate Registration card, the registry-verification chip, and the shared citation strip) along with registry-first retrieval. Nothing about an existing Vendor Intelligence record changes until they are on.

<Note>
  Coverage is honest about its edges. OpenCorporates coverage varies by jurisdiction, SEC EDGAR only covers US registrants, and a DUNS number requires your own entitlement with that provider. In every case the card names which sources were consulted and which were unavailable, rather than presenting a thinner answer as a complete one.
</Note>
