Vendor Intelligence answers two questions about a company without asking the company anything: **is this corporation real and in good standing**, and **is it financially sound enough to depend on**. Both answers are assembled from registries of record and regulatory filings, and every figure on screen names the source it came from.

<Note>
  This is an optional feature currently in beta. Let your Coverbase representative know if you'd like it turned on for your organization. It pairs with [Security Intelligence](/products/security-intelligence), which rates a vendor's external security posture from its own infrastructure.
</Note>

## Corporate registration

Coverbase searches each register by company name, scores every candidate against the vendor's name, and binds a record only on a high-confidence match. A short or ambiguous name (for example, "Meta") binds only when two independent registers agree on the same legal entity.

When nothing matches confidently, the card shows **Needs review** rather than quietly picking the closest hit. That distinction matters: binding the wrong "Acme Holdings" is worse than returning nothing.

| Source | What it establishes |
| ------ | ------------------- |
| **GLEIF** | Authoritative legal name, LEI, entity and registration status, jurisdiction, legal and headquarters addresses |
| **OpenCorporates** | Company number, jurisdiction, incorporation date, current status, link to the source registry |
| **SEC EDGAR** | US public-company validation: CIK, ticker, state of incorporation, SIC industry |
| **Dun & Bradstreet** | DUNS number, where your organization has its own D&B entitlement |

## Financial Health Score

The score runs 0-100, where higher is healthier, and maps to five risk bands from Very High Risk to Very Low Risk. It is designed to land in the same mental model as a RapidRatings FHR, with a different acquisition model behind it: no supplier outreach, portfolio-wide coverage from day one, and a refresh on every fact-sheet run.

### Confidence tiers

The tier is the most important thing on the card. It is derived from the data that actually resolved — never declared — and it travels with the score everywhere the score goes.

| Tier | Backing data | What it means |
| ---- | ------------ | ------------- |
| **A — Filed financials** | Financial statements the company filed with a regulator (SEC EDGAR XBRL) | The full ratio battery. Statement-grade. |
| **B — Estimated financials** | Estimated revenue and EBITDA plus capital structure | Partial ratios computed on estimates. |
| **C — Signals-based** | Funding, headcount, corporate standing, adverse events | Inferred. No financial statements were available. |

<Warning>
  Scores are only directly comparable **within** the same confidence tier. A Tier C 72 and a Tier A 72 are different claims about different evidence. Never rank a Tier A vendor against a Tier C vendor on score alone.
</Warning>

If a vendor's filings stop resolving, it drops to a lower tier and the card says so. It does not keep presenting a statement-grade number built on estimates.

### What the card shows

- **Score, risk band, and confidence tier**, with 3-month and 12-month movement
- **Score breakdown** across five pillars — financial core, capital position, momentum, adverse events, corporate standing — each with the weight it carried
- **Financial vitals**: revenue and growth, operating margin, current ratio, debt/EBITDA, interest coverage, free cash flow margin, estimated runway, total raised, headcount, last funding round
- **Registered entity standing**: status, jurisdiction, and filing compliance
- **Peer context**: the industry cohort the vendor was ranked inside and its percentile within it
- **Score history**, plotted as discrete restatements rather than a smooth line, because each point is a separate computation
- **Adverse events** with their date, review status, and a link to the source that reported each one
- **Score overrides**, with the reason any cap or floor was applied
- **Sources**, each with the date its data was true, plus the sources that were unavailable

### Why this score?

A pillar at 50 is not something you can act on or dispute. Open the rationale on any pillar and the card shows the inputs that produced it — each one with the measured value, what it scored, and how heavily it counted.

A capital position of 50, for example, might resolve to a current ratio of 1.8x scoring 62 at a quarter of the weight, debt/EBITDA of 4.1x scoring 38, and a registration status Coverbase could not determine. That last one matters more than it looks: an undeterminable input is labelled as such rather than shown as a bare number, because "50" reads as measured mediocrity when it actually means nobody knows.

Two things travel with every input:

- **Whether it was peer-ranked.** A ratio scored against the vendor's own industry cohort is a much stronger claim than one scored against an absolute curve. The card says which you're looking at.
- **The date the value was true.** Filed figures lag. An input from a filing two quarters old is labelled with its own as-of date, not the date of the refresh.

The same rationale is available on every factor of the [security rating](/products/security-intelligence#reading-a-finding).

### Peer comparison

A ratio only means something against a comparable set: 4x debt/EBITDA reads very differently at a utility than at a software company. Coverbase ranks each vendor inside its own SIC industry cohort, drawn from the population of filers that reported the same concept in the same period.

When that cohort is too small to support a percentile, the score falls back to a wider group **and says so on the card**. A disclosed wide cohort is honest; an undisclosed one is misleading.

### Adverse events

Events that bear on financial health — bankruptcy filings, going-concern doubt, mass layoffs, auditor changes, delistings, CFO departures, down rounds, material impairments, regulatory actions, late filings — are weighted by severity and decay over time, so a two-year-old event does not weigh the same as last month's.

Events awaiting human confirmation are labeled **Needs review**. Dismissed events stay on the record for audit but stop counting against the score.

## How evidence is sourced

A common assumption is that "web search" means handing a query to a search engine and reading the top results. That is not how Coverbase gathers evidence.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Registries of record are consulted first">
    For any question about a company's identity, GLEIF, OpenCorporates, and SEC EDGAR are queried before any web crawl. Those facts enter as authoritative evidence in the prioritized bucket, ahead of every web result.
  </Step>

  <Step title="The register wins a conflict">
    Where a registry and a web page disagree on a corporate fact, the model is instructed that the registry is correct. A register states a company's legal name and status as a matter of record; a search engine states whatever ranked well today.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Web results are graded before they can be cited">
    Every candidate is classified for relevance to the specific control being evaluated **and** for publisher accountability — authoritative, reputable, or unreliable. Anything below the control set's minimum credibility is discarded, not merely ranked lower.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A control set configured to require authoritative sources will not cite a blog post. It will report that it found nothing, which is the correct answer.

## Availability

Vendor Intelligence is an optional feature in beta, enabled per organization. **Contact your Coverbase representative to have it turned on.**

Corporate registration validation is populated for every organization on each fact-sheet refresh. The Financial Health Score, registry-first retrieval, and the score rationale described above are the parts that require enabling. Nothing about an existing fact sheet changes until they are on.

<Note>
  Coverage is honest about its edges. SEC EDGAR covers roughly 7,000 US filers, so many private vendors score at Tier C. OpenCorporates coverage varies by jurisdiction. DUNS requires your own D&B entitlement. In every case the card names which sources were consulted and which were unavailable, rather than presenting a thinner answer as a complete one.
</Note>
