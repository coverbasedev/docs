Most companies already have their standard written down as a contract: the MSA you send first, the DPA your privacy team maintains, the security addendum you attach to anything touching customer data. **Import from Document** reads one of those and turns it into a clause set, so your playbook starts from language you already use rather than a blank page. The same entry reads a clause library or negotiation playbook; see [Importing a clause library or playbook](#importing-a-clause-library-or-playbook).

<Note>
  This is one of four ways to build a clause set. The others are copying the packaged library, [importing a spreadsheet](/user-guides/clause-set-spreadsheet-import), and adding clauses by hand. They mix freely: generate from your paper, then paste in the two clauses your template forgot.
</Note>

## Which import do I want?

The two upload paths solve different problems.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Import from Document" icon="wand-magic-sparkles">
    **You have a contract.** Coverbase reads the document and proposes the clauses, with the exact source text quoted. Fastest way from "we have a template" to a working set, but every clause arrives with one tier, so you finish the tiering by hand.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Import from Spreadsheet" icon="file-import" href="/user-guides/clause-set-spreadsheet-import">
    **You have a playbook.** Your standards already exist as structured rows, probably with fallback positions. You get exactly what you typed, including full multi-tier variant language, and nothing is inferred.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

A common sequence: generate from your paper to get the clause inventory, then hand-write the fallback tiers in the UI. If your team already maintains a negotiation matrix, skip extraction and import the spreadsheet.

## Run an extraction

<Steps>
  <Step title="Upload the baseline">
    On **Configuration → Clause Sets**, open **Add Clause Set** and choose **Import from Document**. Drop in your template as a PDF, DOCX, DOC, TXT, or MD file.

    Set **Document type** to **Baseline contract (one agreement)**, or leave it on **Detect automatically**. Give the set a **Name**, and optionally a description, then choose **Import**.

    Use a **clean template**, not an executed agreement. A signed contract carries one counterparty's negotiated edits, and you would be baking their redlines into your standard.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Wait for processing">
    Extraction runs in the background; you can leave the page and come back. The clause set appears immediately with a processing indicator and fills in as the run completes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review what it extracted">
    Each proposed clause arrives with a name, category, severity, applicable component types, and the **exact source text** it came from, with a page citation you can check against your document.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add your fallback tiers">
    This is the step people skip, and the set is not usable without it. See [What you have to finish](#what-you-have-to-finish) below.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Activate it">
    Generated clause sets are created **inactive**, so a half-finished playbook cannot be run against a live contract: an inactive set is not offered when you start a review. The Clause Sets page has no switch to activate one yet, so ask your Coverbase contact once the tiers are in.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What you get back

Each extracted clause is a normal reference clause, so you can edit, retier, or archive any of it.

| Field | How it is filled |
| --- | --- |
| **Identifier** | Auto-assigned as `BASE-001`, `BASE-002`, ... Rename them to your own scheme if you have one. |
| **Name** | Proposed from the clause heading. |
| **Category** | Proposed from the clause subject. |
| **Severity** | Proposed from the clause subject. Check these: the model does not know which clauses would block *your* signature. |
| **Applicable component types** | Proposed from the document type it read. |
| **Rationale** and **Guidance** | Proposed where the text supports them: why the clause matters, and a short note on how to compare vendor language against it. |
| **Source evidence** | The exact quoted text plus its page, kept on the clause so a reviewer can trace any standard back to your template. |
| **Variants** | Exactly one: **Tier 1**, labeled **Preferred: baseline contract language**, your template's own language. |

<Note>
  **Language it cannot quote verbatim is dropped, not paraphrased.** Every extracted clause and every piece of source evidence is checked back against the text of the document it cites; anything that does not appear there contiguously is discarded. You may get fewer clauses than you expected, but what you get is your own paper, not a model's summary of it.
</Note>

## What you have to finish

<Warning>
  **A single Tier 1 variant is a floor, not a playbook.** It marks anything that is not your exact template language as needing review, which on a real vendor's paper is almost everything. A set run in that state flags nearly every clause.
</Warning>

Before you point a generated set at a live contract:

1. **Add the tiers you would accept.** For each clause that matters, write the Tier 2 ("acceptable") and Tier 3 ("fallback") language your legal team would sign without escalating.
2. **Fill in the Guidance field.** Extraction may propose a short comparison note, but not the aliases and extraction signals that help the matcher find a clause under a different heading. The [Contract Guardian guide](/user-guides/contract-guardian) shows the four-section format.
3. **Retune severities.** Reserve Critical for clauses that would block a signature.
4. **Archive what you do not care about.** A template contains boilerplate you have no opinion on. Every clause you keep is a clause every review has to triage.
5. **Have it activated**, then test it against a contract you already know before you rely on it.

## Importing a clause library or playbook

Some teams keep their standard as a clause library or negotiation playbook rather than a contract: numbered clauses, each with a preferred position, a fallback, and sometimes a line the team will not cross. **Import from Document** reads that layout as it is written instead of guessing at it. Choose **Clause library / playbook (positions per clause)** under **Document type**, or leave **Detect automatically** on.

| In the document | In the clause set |
| --- | --- |
| Each numbered clause | One reference clause, in the document's order |
| The clause number | The identifier, under the **ID prefix** you set (for example `PB-01`). Left blank, the prefix is the set name's initials. |
| The preferred position | Tier 1 |
| The fallback position | Tier 3 |
| A "do not accept" position, or a clause on the document's "no fallback" list | Tier 5 |
| Negotiation notes, approval routing, and the rules around each clause | The clause's guidance |
| The document's purpose and legend | The set's description, when you leave **Description** blank |

The positions are copied verbatim, never rewritten. Severity is set from the document's own rules and the clause's subject: **Critical** for a clause on the document's "no fallback" list or one whose deviations need executive approval, **High** for one with a floor or a subject such as data, security, or indemnity, **Low** for administrative terms such as notices and governing law, and **Medium** otherwise. Check them as you would any proposed severity.

When the run finishes, the clause set says how many of the document's clauses it imported. Anything the reader could not place, such as a clause with no preferred position or text outside any labeled block, is listed above the clauses under **items need review**. Open those clauses and confirm or fix them.

An imported playbook is created inactive like any other generated set. Under the default scoring, a vendor whose language matches your fallback reads as **Needs review**.

## Troubleshooting

| What you see | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Extraction returned far fewer clauses than the document has | Expected on documents where the text layer is poor. Language that cannot be quoted verbatim is dropped rather than guessed at. Check the PDF is text-based, not a scan of a printout. |
| The clause set is stuck processing | Extraction runs in the background and larger documents take longer. If it stays pending well beyond a few minutes, the run failed. The clause set surfaces the failure, and you can archive it and retry. |
| Clauses came back with your counterparty's edits | You uploaded an executed agreement instead of your template. Start again from clean paper. |
| Everything is marked as a deviation on the first review | The set still has one tier per clause. Add fallback tiers before running it against a live contract. |
| The clause set is not offered when you start a review | Generated sets start inactive, and only active sets are offered. Ask your Coverbase contact to activate it once the tiers are in. |
| A playbook import says fewer clauses were imported than the document has | Open the list under **items need review** on the clause set. It names what the reader could not place. |
