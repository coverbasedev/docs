The **Registrations** tab tells you which legal entity stands behind a vendor, what that rests on, and the corporate group around it. Coverbase reads the vendor's own legal pages, looks the entity up in public registers, and shows you every source it used.

It runs on its own once a vendor has a website on file. You do not start it.

## Finding it

Open a vendor, go to **Vendor Intelligence**, and pick the **Registrations** tab.

## What the entity rests on

The label above the entity's name says what the identity rests on. Read it before you use any identifier below it.

| Label | What it means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bound to a register** | A public register holds this entity, and the evidence agrees with your vendor. | Use the identifiers. They are safe to quote in a questionnaire. |
| **Identified from the vendor's own pages** | No open register holds the entity, but the vendor names it on its own legal pages. | Read the quotes. Confirm the entity, add its registration number, or ask the vendor. |
| **Confirmed by your team** | Someone in your organization named or picked the entity. | Nothing. Every refresh keeps it. |
| **Match result: needs review** | Several registered companies could be your vendor. | Pick the right one from **Possible matches**. |
| **Recorded by your team** | Someone in your organization recorded that no registered entity stands behind the vendor. | Nothing. Coverbase stops searching the registers under its name. |
| **No registry match** | Nothing credible matched, and the vendor's pages name no entity. | Normal for small, new, or non-corporate suppliers. Use **Correct with AI** if you know the entity, or **Mark as no registered entity** if there is none. |

## How Coverbase finds the entity

Coverbase looks for the entity the way you would if you searched for it yourself.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Read the vendor's own legal pages">
    The terms, privacy policy, imprint and site footer name the company a customer contracts with, often with its registration number, its former names and its subsidiaries. Coverbase reads them and keeps the passage each entity came from. A company named only in passing, such as a payment provider or a hosting company, is ignored.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Look the entity up by its number">
    When the vendor states a registration number, Coverbase looks it up directly in GLEIF and Companies House. The record is bound only when its name agrees with the one the vendor states.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Search the registers by name">
    Coverbase searches GLEIF, Companies House, OpenCorporates and SEC EDGAR for the stated legal name, the vendor's name and its former names. A record that carries the exact legal name the vendor states wins over one that only shares its brand. A record in another country than the vendor's is offered for review, not bound.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fall back to the vendor's word">
    When no register holds the entity, the page shows the entity the vendor names, marked **Identified from the vendor's own pages**, with the quotes. It stays unvalidated until you confirm it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Identifiers

Under the verdict, each identifier has a tile: the LEI, the company number with the register that issued it, the SEC CIK, and the D-U-N-S number. A dashed tile is one no register returned.

- **Add a D-U-N-S number** saves the number Dun & Bradstreet assigns to the vendor.
- **Add a file number**, **Add HRB number** or **Add company number** appears when the entity rests on the vendor's word. Coverbase looks the number up wherever the home register is open.

When a register holds the entity, **How we identified it** shows the three steps that got there: your vendor record, the passage on the vendor's own pages, and the register record. Each step links to its source.

## Confirm an entity the vendor names

When the entity is **Identified from the vendor's own pages**, **What would confirm it** lists the evidence that would settle it.

- **Confirm** saves the entity as your team's, for example once you have checked it against your signed contract.
- **Ask the vendor** drafts a message asking which entity contracts with you and for its registration number. **Request a certificate of incorporation** drafts a request for the certificate. Edit the message, copy it, and send it from your own mail.

## Organization structure

**Organization structure** shows the vendor's parents above it and its subsidiaries below it. Each entity shows its country, its identifiers, and where it is known from:

- **GLEIF** or **Companies House**: a register holds the entity and reports the relationship.
- **Named by the vendor**: only the vendor's own pages mention the entity. Treat the link as the vendor's statement. Click **Add HRB number** (or the number its country uses) to add its registration number.

When a group has many subsidiaries, filter them by country. GLEIF lists at most 25 here, and the heading gives the register's total.

## Choose among possible matches

When several registered companies share your vendor's name, **Possible matches** lists them, ranked by what agrees besides the name: the state or country, the city, and the industry. Click **Use this entity** on the right one. The page binds it at once, and every later refresh keeps your choice.

If none of them is your vendor, click **Correct with AI** to name the right entity, or **Mark as no registered entity** when the vendor has none, such as a sole trader or an open-source project.

## Correct with AI

Use **Correct with AI** when the entity is wrong or missing. You describe the right entity in your own words, and you see the match before anything changes.

1. Click **Correct with AI** in the verdict.
2. Describe the entity. For example: "Our contract is with PostHog's UK company, Hiberly Ltd." You can also paste a registration number, an LEI, or the signature block from your contract.
3. Click **Find the entity**. Coverbase reads your note, searches the registers, and shows what it found and the proposed change.
4. Click **Apply** to save it, or **Not right, try again** to edit your note.
5. The panel confirms the change. Click **Back to registrations**, or **Undo** to put the previous entity back.

## Undo a change

Every change you make on the page, a picked match, a confirmed entity, an added number, or a correction, shows a banner at the top with **Undo**. Undo restores what your team had confirmed before, or returns the vendor to what the evidence alone shows.

## Registered address

The address on the register is the vendor's legal address, not where its people work. A registered agent's or accountant's address is normal and is not a red flag by itself.

## What to do with a dissolved status

A **dissolved** status on a vendor you are paying needs attention. It usually means a restructure moved the business to another entity, or the company on your contract no longer exists. Ask procurement which legal entity is on the current contract, and compare it to the registered name on this page.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| The page says no registration record yet | The vendor has no website on file. Add one, and the check runs on the next refresh. |
| The legal name differs from the name you use | Trading names and registered names often differ. The **Also known as** list shows both. |
| A private US company stays **Identified from the vendor's own pages** | US state registers are not open to search, so the entity cannot be checked against one. Confirm it, or ask the vendor for its state file number. |
| The vendor's pages name no entity | Some sites block automated reading or build their pages in the browser. Use **Correct with AI** with the entity on your contract. |
| A match was bound but you disagree | Use **Correct with AI** and name the right entity. |
| A US vendor has no CIK | Only public filers have one. Its absence says nothing about the company. |
