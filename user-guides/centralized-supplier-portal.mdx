Before this existed, everything you asked a supplier for arrived as its own link in its own email. A supplier with nine open items had nine links in nine threads, no way to see them together, and no way to tell you something had changed unless you happened to ask.

The centralized portal gives each supplier one address. They sign in with their work email, see everything you have open with them, bring their own colleagues in, and keep the details you hold about them current without waiting to be asked.

<Note>
  Turning it on changes nothing about links you have already sent. Every `/portal/…` link keeps working exactly as it did.
</Note>

## Turning it on

Go to **Configuration › Portal › Centralized Portal** and switch it on.

The page lists what will change before you flip it, not after. Four things happen:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Suppliers get one page">
    They sign in and see every request you have open with them, grouped by kind.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Suppliers bring their own people">
    The first person at the supplier to sign in becomes the owner. They can invite colleagues and assign work to them without going through you.
  </Step>
  <Step title="New invitations lead with the portal">
    The button in an invitation or reminder points at the portal. The single-request link stays in the email underneath it, for anyone who would rather not sign in.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Everything open appears in it">
    Including requests you sent before you turned this on. Their own links keep working too.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Naming your portal

**Portal Title**, in the **Branding and Policies** card, is what your suppliers see the portal called, for example "Halstead Third-Party Risk Portal". It heads their home page, names the browser tab, and appears on the sign-in screen, including the one on your own portal domain if you have one. Leave it empty and the portal uses your organization's name. It can be up to 120 characters.

The page saves each change as you make it and says so at the top right. The **Preview** beside the settings draws the portal as a supplier will see it: your logo and accent color, the title, the sections you have on, and the footer.

## Choosing sections

Home, requests, documents, messages and team are always there. Four more are yours to switch on, each with a switch under **Sections**. The settings that belong to a section appear beneath it once it is on.

## What a supplier may change without being asked

With **Profile** on, **Updates Without a Request** decides which parts of their own record a supplier may revise at any time. Nothing is open by default.

| Scope | What it opens |
| --- | --- |
| **PO Email Address** | Where purchase orders and remittance advice are emailed, on each address they hold with you. |
| **Addresses** | Adding, editing and retiring their own sites. |
| **Bank Accounts** | Adding an account, or replacing one. Waits for your approval while bank review is on. |
| **Tax Registrations** | Registration numbers and exemption declarations. |
| **Diversity Declarations** | Inclusion categories and business size. |

Two scopes cannot be put here at all, and a note under the switches says so:

- **Contact details**
- **Your legal entities**

Those are yours to initiate. If you want either updated, send an update request naming that section, and it opens for that supplier for the life of the request.

<Warning>
  The standing scope and an open request's scope add together, they do not narrow each other. A supplier with PO emails standing open, who is then sent a request asking for a new bank account, may do both until that request closes.
</Warning>

### Saying which part needs updating

Update requests are sent through the Coverbase API, with `POST /v1/vendors/{vendor_id}/update_requests`. The dashboard has no button for them. A request names the sections it covers and can carry a message; with no sections named, it uses **Sections to request by default** from the **Update requests** settings in [Know Your Vendor settings](/user-guides/supplier-information#configuring-it-for-your-organization), and it expires after **Request expires after (days)**.

On the supplier's **Profile**, each section a request names is marked as one you asked about, and they can revise it until the request closes or expires.

## What waits for your approval

A new or changed bank account waits for a person while **Bank changes need a reviewer** is on in [Know Your Vendor settings](/user-guides/supplier-information#configuring-it-for-your-organization), which it is by default. A legal entity assignment waits when **Legal entity assignments need approval** is on there. Everything else a supplier submits from **Profile** (addresses, PO email addresses, tax registrations and diversity declarations) is checked against their country's rules and saved to their record straight away, and the supplier's **Changes** tab tells them so.

You decide bank changes where you already review them: the pending changes on the vendor's **Know-Your-Vendor** tab, or the **Vendor changes** page. Approving writes the change and fires the webhook your finance system listens to. Rejecting lets you give a reason, and the supplier sees it on their **Changes** tab.

## Findings and obligations

With **Obligations and Findings** on, choose what a supplier sees under **Items Suppliers See**:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Only what you have raised with them" icon="shield-check">
    A finding appears once you have created a commitment against it. Recommended: it means nothing reaches a supplier before you decided to ask them about it.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Every open item on their record" icon="eye">
    Every open finding and obligation for that supplier, whether or not you have asked them about it.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Either way a supplier sees a finding's title, severity, source, status and due date, and what you asked them to do. An obligation shows its type (**Complementary control** or **Shared responsibility**) where a finding shows severity. Analyst notes, severity reasoning and evaluation extracts are never in the list.

## Letting suppliers start their own requests

With **Submissions** on, tick the templates a supplier may start themselves under **Templates They May Start**: a security incident notification, a change-of-control notice, an insurance certificate refresh.

A template appears in that list only after you turn on **Let suppliers start this themselves** in the template editor. Both have to be true, so archiving a template stops offering it without you editing anything here.

What a supplier starts is an ordinary request. It gets a reference number, lands in your queue, carries its own asks, uploads, messages and reminders, and appears wherever your team already watches for work.

## Sending a template on a schedule

In the template editor's **Centralized Portal** block, pick a cadence under **Send On A Schedule**: every month, or every 3, 6, 12 or 24 months. Leave it on **Only when someone sends it** and nothing changes.

Then pick supplier tags under **Send It To**. Only suppliers carrying one of those tags receive the template. With no tags picked nothing goes out: an empty audience means nobody, not everybody.

A daily sweep sends the template to every supplier in that audience with a portal whose last request from it is older than the cadence. Templates on a cadence are listed under **Scheduled Requests** on the Centralized Portal tab. Two things worth knowing:

- A supplier who still has that template open is **skipped, not stacked**. An annual questionnaire nobody answered would otherwise become two open requests, then twelve.
- The clock runs from the send, not from the answer. A supplier who never answers is still asked again on schedule.

## Branding, policies and security

**Branding and Policies** applies to every page a supplier answers a request on, as well as the portal title above:

- **Confidentiality Footer**: text shown at the foot of each answering page. Leave it empty for no footer.
- **Privacy Policy Link** and **Trust Center Link**: shown as links in that footer. Each must be a full link starting with `https://`, and the page says so before saving one that is not.
- Your logo and accent color come from **Organization settings**, not from here.

Under **Security**, **Require a one-time passcode** makes a request link ask for a code emailed to the address it was sent to before anything is shown.

## Where to see whether a supplier has one

Open the vendor and go to its **Portals** tab. The **Centralized Portal** card at the top says whether that supplier has a portal and whether it is **Open** or **Closed**, links straight to it with **Open portal**, and holds three tabs:

- **Open Requests**: everything you have open with them right now.
- **People**: who at the supplier can sign in.
- **Shared Documents**: what you have shared onto their **Documents** page. The tab appears when **Share documents back to them** is on.

Everything you have ever sent them, open or closed, is in **All Requests** under the card. A supplier has no portal until you send them their first request.

## Pausing, rescheduling or closing one request

**Open Requests** lists each open request with its reference, its kind and when you sent it. Overdue and soonest due come first, and paused requests last. Click a title to open the request. The menu at the end of a row has everything else:

<Frame caption="One request's menu.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/central-portal-request-menu.png" alt="Row menu with Change due date, Pause request and Close request" />
</Frame>

| Action | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Change due date** | Moves the due date. Reminders follow the new date. |
| **Pause request** | Puts the request on hold. No reminders go out for it, and the supplier sees it marked **On hold**. They can still open it and answer. |
| **Resume request** | Takes it off hold. The due date moves on by the days it was paused, and you can pick a different date in the same step. |
| **Close request** | The supplier can no longer open or submit it, and its reminders stop. Their portal and their other requests are untouched. You can reopen it from **All Requests**. |

Pausing is for work you still want, just not now, for example while a contract is renegotiated. Close a request you no longer need.

<Frame caption="Resuming a request that was paused for 14 days.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/central-portal-resume.png" alt="Resume this request dialog with a due date moved on by 14 days" />
</Frame>

The supplier sees the hold on their **Requests** page and on the request itself:

<Frame caption="What the supplier sees.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/central-portal-supplier-on-hold.png" alt="Supplier's request page with a notice that the customer has put the request on hold" />

## Acting on several requests at once

Tick the box on a row to select that request. Hold Shift and tick another to select every request between the two. Once something is selected, clicking anywhere on a row adds it to the selection or takes it out, instead of opening it. The box above the list selects them all.

A bar then appears at the foot of the page with what applies to your selection: **Pause**, **Resume** and **Close**, each with a count. **Pause** leaves out requests already on hold, and **Resume** counts only the paused ones. Each asks you to confirm first.

<Frame caption="Three open requests selected, and the bar that acts on them.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/central-portal-bulk-requests.png" alt="Open Requests with three of four requests ticked, and a bar offering Pause 3 and Close 3" />
</Frame>

The **All Requests** table under the card selects the same way, and its bar can also reopen closed requests.

## Deciding who can sign in

**People** lists everyone you or the supplier invited, with their role and whether they have signed in yet. Use **Add someone** to invite a person as an **Owner** or a **Contributor**. An owner can invite their own colleagues; a contributor can only answer.

<Frame caption="The People tab with one person's menu open.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/central-portal-people.png" alt="People tab listing an invited contributor, two people who signed in, and one person removed" />
</Frame>

Each person's menu has:

- **Make owner** or **Make contributor**.
- **Resend invitation**, for someone who has not signed in yet.
- **Remove access**, which asks you to confirm first.

Someone you remove can no longer sign in to the supplier's portal or open anything in it, and the supplier's own owner cannot add them back. Your team can: open the removed list at the foot of **People** and click **Restore access**.

<Warning>
  Removing someone does not recall a link to a single request that was already emailed to them. That link keeps working until you close that request.
</Warning>

## Closing one supplier's portal

To shut a supplier's portal without turning the feature off for everyone, open the menu next to **Open portal** and choose **Close portal**.

<Frame caption="Closing a portal. The requests inside stay open unless you tick the box.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/central-portal-close.png" alt="Close Microsoft's portal dialog with an option to also close its four open requests" />
</Frame>

While it is closed:

- The supplier's people see that it is closed and cannot open anything in it.
- No reminders go out, for the portal or for the requests in it.
- Nothing is deleted.

Tick the box to close each open request too, which also stops their single-request links working. Leave it off and those requests stay open, and each one's own link keeps working, until you close them one by one.

The card then says who closed the portal and when, and **Reopen portal** puts everything back as it was.

<Frame caption="A closed portal.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/central-portal-closed.png" alt="Centralized Portal card marked Closed with the name of who closed it and a Reopen portal button" />
</Frame>

To close several suppliers' portals at once, go to **Configuration › Portal › All Portals**. Tick the portals under **Supplier portals** (Shift and a row click select the same way as above), then choose **Close** in the bar. The same box offers to close their open requests too.

<Frame caption="Closing from All Portals.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/central-portal-close-several.png" alt="Close this portal dialog with an option to also close its four open requests" />
</Frame>

## Turning it off

The switch is reversible and loses nothing. The customer disappears from each supplier's list, and every `/portal/…` link you have already sent keeps working. Turn it back on and the requests, documents, messages and members are exactly as they were.

A supplier who had access and finds it gone is told what happened rather than being offered a way to ask for access back, because nobody revoked them and their own owner cannot undo this.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| A supplier says reminders stopped | Look at the vendor's **Portals** tab. A **Paused** badge on the request, or a **Closed** portal, both stop reminders. |
| The due date moved when I resumed a request | Resuming adds the days it was paused. Pick another date in the **Resume request** dialog, or use **Change due date** afterwards. |
| Someone I removed still opened a request | They used a single-request link emailed before you removed them. Close that request. |
| Someone I removed is back on the portal | Only your team can restore a person you removed. Check **People** for who restored them, or remove them again. |
| Closing the portal did not close its requests | That is the default. Close them from each request's menu, or tick the box to close them when you close the portal. |
| There are no boxes to tick on the requests | Your role cannot change portals. Ask an admin for a role that can. |
| There is no Centralized Portal card on the vendor | Either the centralized portal is off for your organization, or you have not sent this supplier a request yet. |
