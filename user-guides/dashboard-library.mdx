A dashboard in Coverbase is a grid of charts drawn from live workspace data across vendors, assessments, findings, contracts and intake requests. You can build one chart at a time, but most reporting questions have already been answered: the library ships **15 ready-made dashboards** and **110 individual charts**, each written for a specific job rather than for a table in the database.

Open them from **Dashboards**: **Browse templates** starts a whole dashboard, and **Chart library** on any open dashboard adds individual charts to it. Clicking a dashboard card creates that dashboard straight away, under the template's name, and opens it.

<Note>
  Applying a template makes a copy you own. The charts and the dashboard become ordinary records in your workspace, editable like any other. Retitle them, change the measure, re-slice them, resize them, delete them. Nothing syncs back: later changes to the Coverbase template never reach your copy, and your edits never leave your workspace.
</Note>

## The chart library

The dashboards above draw on 93 of the library's charts. Every one of them is also available on its own, along with 17 more that no packaged dashboard uses. Open **Chart library** from a dashboard, search or pick a shelf, select as many as you want, and they are added below whatever is already on the grid, so an add never lands on top of a widget you positioned.

Charts are filed by the question they answer, not by the table the data comes from, so a spend chart and a vendor chart can sit on the same shelf if they answer the same question.

<Frame caption="The chart library. Shelves across the top, cards in the middle, and the selected chart drawn against your own data on the right.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/dashboard-chart-library.png" alt="The Coverbase chart library dialog, showing the search field, category shelves with counts, chart cards, and a live preview of the selected chart" />
</Frame>

Selecting a card renders it against your own data in the panel beside the grid, so you can see a real result before you commit it to the dashboard. The preview covers all time; once the chart is added, the dashboard's own date range applies. You can select several and add them in one go.

<Frame caption="Chart cards in the library. Each one previews the shape of the chart, says what it shows, and lists the jobs it is meant for.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/dashboard-chart-cards.png" alt="Four chart cards from the Coverbase chart library, each with a preview thumbnail, description and job labels" />
</Frame>

Search matches names, descriptions and the job labels on each card, so **renewals**, **board reporting** and **SLA** all find something. It also matches the vocabulary your workspace uses: if you have renamed "vendor" to "supplier", searching for suppliers finds the vendor charts.

## What you will and will not see

The library hides templates your workspace cannot fill, so you never add a chart that can only ever render empty.

| These charts need | Which means | If it is off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Contracts** | The Contracts module enabled, and permission to read contracts | The 26 spend and savings charts are hidden, along with Contract Spend & Renewals, Savings & Value Capture and Third-Party Exposure |
| **Intake** | Permission to read intake requests | The 4 intake charts are hidden, along with Intake & Demand |
| **Radar** | The Radar module enabled, and permission to read Radar alerts or detectors | The 11 Radar signal charts are hidden, along with Radar Signal Triage |
| **Inspect** | The Inspect module enabled, and permission to read integrations | Inspect Issues by Application is hidden |
| **Risk register** | The Risk module enabled, and permission to read risks | Risks by Domain and Appetite is hidden |
| **Residual risk** | Residual risk scoring turned on for the workspace | Vendors by Residual Risk Level is hidden |

A dashboard inherits its charts' requirements: if any chart on it needs Contracts, the whole dashboard is hidden from workspaces without Contracts rather than arriving with holes in it. A shelf with nothing left to show drops out of the row of shelves.

The remaining 66 charts and 10 dashboards depend on nothing beyond vendors, assessments and findings, so every workspace sees them.

## Making them yours

A template is a starting point, not a fixed report. Once a chart is on your dashboard:

- **Retitle it.** The shipped names are descriptive, not fixed. "Median Assessment Cycle Time" may be "Time to clear a vendor" in your program.
- **Change the slice.** Most charts group by one dimension and split by another. Swapping either (tag, department, analyst, risk tier, quarter) usually gets you a different question answered by the same chart.
- **Resize and rearrange.** Drag any widget; the grid reflows around it.
- **Set the date range** on the dashboard rather than per chart. Every chart on it follows.
- **Mix shelves freely.** Nothing stops a savings chart sitting next to a cycle-time chart if that is the story your review needs.

<Warning>
  Charts read live data with your permissions applied, so two people can open the same dashboard and see different totals. Before circulating a number from a dashboard, check that your audience can see the same scope you can.
</Warning>
