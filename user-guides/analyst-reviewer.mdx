It assumes your environment is already configured — if you're the one setting it up, start with the [Admin & Setup guide](/user-guides/admin-setup).
</Info>

Welcome. Coverbase reads vendor documents and measures them against your controls, so your job shifts from hunting through PDFs to **judging results**. A typical assessment measures a hundred-plus controls and raises just a handful of material issues — in one real example, 111 controls produced 5.

No prior Coverbase experience needed. Here's the loop you'll run:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Triage intake">Review enriched new-vendor requests.</Step>
  <Step title="Launch assessment">Attach control sets and let analysis run.</Step>
  <Step title="Review issues">Judge the material items, evidence in hand.</Step>
  <Step title="Correct the AI">Fix wrong or noisy results — permanently.</Step>
  <Step title="Follow up">Ask vendors only about genuine gaps.</Step>
  <Step title="Disposition & report">Decide, complete, and export.</Step>
  <Step title="Watch Radar">Turn monitoring signal into action.</Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Two habits make the whole thing work: **validate against source language** (every result carries citations — click through), and **correct the AI when it's wrong** (corrections are permanent, so the issue count drops with every cycle).
</Tip>

## Step 1: Triage new vendor requests

**What this does:** New requests arrive from business requesters already **enriched** — sanctions, financials, security posture, and a redundancy check against your existing portfolio — with most IRQ questions auto-answered by the research agent.

**How to do it:**

1. Open the pending submission and scan the **enrichment readout**. A red flag on sanctions is a fuzzy match to investigate; an unknown vendor with no public presence also flags red.
2. Check the **redundancy readout** — if the vendor (or a comparable tool) already exists in your portfolio, this may be a new service on an existing record rather than a new onboarding.
3. Review the **auto-answered IRQ**. A "limited information" flag means the AI couldn't confirm authoritatively — not that a problem exists. Click the **reviewed** checkbox on each answer you validate; your name and timestamp are recorded.
4. Answer anything flagged for **manual input** (typically the judgment questions your admin marked Required).
5. If something's ambiguous, use **Request Clarification** to send a templated email back to the requester before approving.
6. If the requester misidentified an existing vendor, approve the submission and use the **vendor record merge** function to consolidate it under the parent record.
7. **Accept** to kick off onboarding — the right assessment plan attaches automatically based on tags and inherent risk.

</Note>

## Step 2: Launch an assessment

**What this does:** Opens the review — attaches control sets, kicks off document collection, and runs the analysis.

**How to do it:**

1. Open the vendor record → **Assessments → New Assessment**. Choose **vendor-level** or **service-level** scope. Multiple assessments can coexist, and one assessment can carry multiple control sets at once (e.g., SIG core plus your bespoke InfoSec set).
2. Confirm or select the control sets and assessment plan.
3. Let **automatic evidence collection** run. Coverbase pulls first from its library of vendor-supplied compliance documents, then scrapes the vendor's trust center for anything ungated (form-filling access requests for gated documents), before falling back to asking the vendor.
4. Where the vendor must be engaged, send the **portal invitation** with a due date — reminders and the pre-signed MNDA your admin configured go out automatically.
5. Analysis typically completes in roughly **5–10 minutes** once evidence is in; large control sets (a full SIG core is ~300 questions) take longer.

<Frame caption="The assessment workspace — control sets attached in the left rail, and the lifecycle (Collected documents → Analyzed controls → Reviewed results) tracked as it runs.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/analyst-assessment-summary.png" alt="Assessment workspace showing control sets and lifecycle" />
</Frame>

<Note>
  **Worth knowing:** A vendor uploading a blank or junk document doesn't slip through — the AI analysis fails it automatically. Trust-center scraping lands documents about 20–30% of the time; the portal remains the primary path for smaller and historical vendors.
</Note>

## Step 3: Review the results

**What this does:** The AI has measured every document against every control and raised **issues** where evidence is missing or contradicts the expectation. Your job is judgment, in a deliberate order.

**How to do it:**

1. Start in the **Critical Findings** view, not the full issue list — triage material items first.
2. Toggle **Assign to Me** to filter to your controls. Issues can be assigned per risk domain (InfoSec to InfoSec SMEs, and so on) or round-robin across the team.
3. Open each issue: you'll see the **expectation**, the **guidance** the AI used, and the **analysis** citing the specific evidence. Click through to the source language to validate — never take the summary on faith for a material item.
4. **Spot-check compliant controls too**, especially early on. Passes show the same full evidence chain (plus web-sourced supporting articles), and this is how you catch false negatives while you build trust in the output.
5. Where two control sets overlap (e.g., SIG core and the out-of-box SOC 2 set raising the same gap twice), mark the duplicate as **not an issue** and tell your admin to drop the redundant set from future assessments.

## Step 4: Correct the AI

**What this does:** Turns a wrong or noisy result into a **permanent** improvement. You edit the control's guidance, rerun it on the spot, and — if you accept — the change applies to every future assessment.

**How to do it:**

1. On the issue, choose **Correct the AI**.
2. Write the missing condition into the guidance — be specific.
3. **Rerun** the control and confirm the result changes as expected.
4. **Accept** to save globally, or discard.

<Frame caption="Correct the AI — edit the guidance, rerun the control, and accept to make the fix permanent across all future assessments.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/analyst-correct-ai.png" alt="Correct the AI guidance editor" />
</Frame>

<Accordion title="Real corrections from live reviews" icon="wand-magic-sparkles">
  - **Clean desk policy** flagged for a SaaS vendor → added "mark fully compliant if the vendor is not a professional services or contracting company with a physical on-site presence" → rerun resolved to no issue.
  - **SOC 2 currency** → tightened the default 12-month window to 6 months on the control — and the draft vendor follow-up regenerated itself to reflect the new window.
  - **Employment contractual agreements** → added "accept an information security training attestation as sufficient evidence" → issue resolved and saved for all future assessments.
</Accordion>

<Warning>
  **Be specific, not lenient.** Distinguish a public company from a startup, a SaaS vendor from professional services, rather than broadly relaxing a control. Specific guidance is what makes an exception defensible to a regulator — and what keeps the same false positive from ever coming back.
</Warning>

## Step 5: Follow up with the vendor

**What this does:** For genuine gaps, sends the vendor a **contextualized** question — referencing what they already submitted — through the portal.

**How to do it:**

1. On an issue, choose **Draft Follow-up** and use **AI Autofill** — it writes the question in context ("we reviewed your SOC 2 and it doesn't address X") rather than pasting raw control text, which vendors respond to far better.
2. For volume: select all issues → **Create Follow-ups → AI Autofill** runs across the batch → set the portal due date and send in one step.
3. Pick the email template and add any vendor-specific instructions; the internal requester is CC'd automatically.
4. The vendor answers and uploads new evidence directly in the portal; responses flow back onto the issues.

<Frame caption="Draft Follow-up with AI Autofill — a question written in the context of what the vendor already submitted.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/analyst-draft-followup.png" alt="Draft follow-up with AI autofill" />
</Frame>

<Tip>
  **Pace yourself.** Go one-by-one for your first assessments — reviewing each drafted follow-up is how you tune guidance and tone. Switch to bulk once you trust the output.
</Tip>

## Step 6: Disposition, complete, and report

**What this does:** Closes the review — every remaining issue gets a decision, the vendor gets a status and a reassessment date, and the record becomes a report.

### Your three dispositions per issue

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Accept the risk" icon="circle-check">
    Records a timestamped rationale on the record, audit-backed.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Keep the follow-up open" icon="clock">
    The issue stays pending vendor response.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Promote to a finding" icon="flag">
    **Action → Add Finding**, with AI autofill for consistent language; assign a named owner and a due date. Findings persist beyond the assessment.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

### Then complete the assessment

1. Set the **onboarding status** (custom statuses supported) and your **recommendation**.
2. Confirm the **reassessment timeline** — it auto-sets from the risk rating but is fully overridable; anything unresolved converts to findings, not deleted.
3. **Export the report:** Standard PDF for most reviews, Executive Summary for leadership, or Full (every control with all evidence) when an examiner asks. Your organization's branded template fills itself automatically.

## Step 7: Contracts and clause reviews

**What this does:** The contracts module extracts key terms from MSAs, SOWs, and DPAs — value, effective date, term and renewal, indemnity, billing, data return — and compares vendor paper against your preferred language.

**How to do it:**

1. Upload the contract to the vendor's **Documents**, setting the document type manually for MSAs, SOWs, and order forms.
2. Open the **Contracts** module to review extracted terms and the clause inventory — or just ask in natural language: "what does this agreement say about insurance?" queries across all of the vendor's agreements at once.
3. Run a **clause review** against your organization's clause sets — the output shows deltas from your acceptable / fallback / unacceptable tiers, by severity. This is materiality analysis, not redlining; actual redlines stay in Word.
4. Generate follow-up drafts for negotiation points, export the review to Excel, or assign it to legal in-platform.

## Step 8: Work Radar alerts

**What this does:** Radar watches external sources for events touching your third and fourth parties. Your job is to turn signal into action — and keep noise out of the program.

**How to do it:**

1. Review the **alerts feed** for your detectors. Fourth-party mapping means one event (say, a compromised identity provider) surfaces every vendor exposed through it.
2. Open a **case** on anything worth investigating — a case is preliminary, not yet a confirmed problem — and tag the affected vendors.
3. Send a **vendor inquiry** from the case if you need their confirmation or remediation plan.
4. **Close** the case if the vendor is unaffected; **promote** it to a finding (owner + due date) if confirmed; **trigger an off-cycle reassessment** straight from the event when warranted.
5. If a detector keeps firing on non-actionable items, flag it — detector guidance and severity thresholds are quick admin fixes.

## Frequently asked questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="How long does analysis take?">
    Roughly 5–10 minutes for a typical assessment once evidence is collected; very large control sets (a full SIG core is ~300 questions) run longer. Issue counts drop over time as guidance is tuned — the first-pass reduction is typically around 80% versus reviewing every control by hand.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Will the same assessment give identical results twice?">
    Not exactly — AI models are non-deterministic, so two runs can differ slightly at the margins. Extraction is deliberately tuned to over-surface rather than miss. Specific control guidance narrows the variance; results are stable on well-tuned sets.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What if the AI is wrong?">
    That's what review is for — every result carries citations to source language, so errors are visible fast. Use **Correct the AI** to fix it permanently rather than just dismissing it; your corrections become the record.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Do vendors actually respond to the portal?">
    A bare portal invite lands around 50%. Contextualized follow-ups, the pre-signed MNDA, and automatic reminder cadences push that meaningfully higher — and the automated collection paths mean many vendors never need to be asked at all.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can Coverbase get documents from gated trust centers?">
    It downloads anything ungated automatically and form-fills access requests for gated documents. It doesn't log into credentialed portals or read your inbox — documents that arrive by email get uploaded manually.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What file formats work best?">
    PDF — citations and evidence highlighting are strongest there. DOCX is supported for extraction; download web-based terms and upload them for parsing.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
