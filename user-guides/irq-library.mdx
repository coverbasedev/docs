An inherent risk questionnaire (IRQ) rates a third party *before* any of their controls are taken into account. Its score decides how much due diligence the relationship earns and how often it comes back for reassessment, so it is short: the longest template here is fifteen questions and most are ten to twelve, and the shortest takes about a minute.

The **IRQ library** gives you twenty-one of them ready to use, grouped into cybersecurity, compliance, financial, operations, legal, reputational and sector templates. Open it from the **IRQ Library** button at the top right of **Configuration → Questionnaires → Inherent Risk**, browse by category, and copy the one you want.

<Note>
  Copying a template produces an ordinary questionnaire you own and can edit: question wording, options, weights, reviewers, everything. Nothing syncs back: later changes to the Coverbase template do not reach your copy, and your edits never leave your workspace.
</Note>

## Choosing a starting point

Pick by **what the engagement does to your risk**, not by the industry you are in. A hospital buying a marketing analytics tool wants the Data Privacy set, not the Healthcare one, unless that tool will touch patient records.

## Every template in detail

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Standard Inherent Risk Questionnaire · 15 questions · General" icon="scale-balanced">
    The industry-agnostic default. It covers data sensitivity and volume, network access, business criticality, customer contact, contract spend, regulatory exposure, offshoring, subcontracting and replaceability.

    **Engagement Overview** *(no risk domain; context only, unscored)*

    1. Describe the product or service the third party will provide. *(free text)*
    2. Who is the internal relationship or business owner for this engagement? *(contacts)*

    **Data Access and Sensitivity** *(Cybersecurity)*

    3. Will the third party access, store, transmit or process any of our data?
    4. What is the most sensitive classification of data involved? Options: Public or de-identified · Private · Confidential · Restricted
    5. How many records will the third party access, store, transmit or process in aggregate? Options: five bands from under 1,000 to 500,000 or more
    6. Will the third party have access to our networks or internal systems? Options: via their own equipment · via company equipment or VDI · no
    7. Is this a hosted or cloud-delivered solution?

    **Business Impact** *(no risk domain)*

    8. How critical is this product or service to the business? Options: High, unrecoverable process · Medium, recoverable with minor impact · Low
    9. Does the third party have direct or indirect contact with our customers?
    10. Does the third party support our critical infrastructure (software, hardware, data center or network connectivity)?
    11. How long would it take to replace this third party if needed? Options: over 12 months · 6-12 months · under 6 months
    12. What is the expected spend over the life of the contract? Options: six bands from under US$5,000 to over US$1,000,000

    **Regulatory and Geographic Exposure** *(Compliance)*

    13. Does this relationship present a regulatory risk? Options: Yes · Unknown · No
    14. Is any aspect of this product or service, including subcontracted work, performed offshore? Options: offshore with offshore data · offshore with onshore data · no
    15. Does the third party rely on a material subcontractor for any part of the product or service?

    **Heaviest weights:** offshore processing and contract spend, each up to 20 points. **No score floors.** The answers alone set the score.

    **Typical use:** New vendor intake triage; determining due diligence depth; setting reassessment cadence; annual re-tiering.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Inherent Risk Quick Triage · 6 questions · General" icon="bolt">
    Written for a requester who is not a risk professional, so a software purchase request can be routed in under a minute. Six questions, one section, no free text.

    1. What is the most sensitive data this vendor will handle? Options: Regulated or restricted (health, financial, government ID) · Confidential business or customer records · Internal only · Public only · None
    2. Will the vendor connect to our systems, networks or accounts? Options: privileged/admin/write · read-only or limited integration · no
    3. If this vendor went down for a week, what would happen? Options: could not serve customers · a team significantly degraded · little or no impact
    4. What is the expected annual spend? Options: over US$250,000 · US$25,000-250,000 · under US$25,000
    5. Does the engagement fall under a regulation such as GDPR, HIPAA, PCI DSS, GLBA or SOX? Options: Yes · Unknown · No
    6. Will the vendor interact with our customers or process customer data? Options: directly · indirectly · no

    **No score floors.** Every question is weighted evenly enough that no single answer dominates, which is what you want from a form a requester fills in about themselves.

    **Typical use:** Self-service vendor intake; fast-track approval for low-risk vendors; pre-screening before a full questionnaire.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Financial Services Inherent Risk · 13 questions · Financial" icon="landmark">
    Built around the pivotal determination in the [2023 Interagency Guidance on Third-Party Relationships](https://www.federalregister.gov/d/2023-12340): whether the relationship supports a *critical activity*.

    **Criticality and Regulatory Designation** *(Compliance)*

    1. Does this relationship support a critical activity, meaning one that could cause significant risk if the third party fails to perform, significant customer impact, or significant impact on operations? **Yes floors the score at 70.**
    2. Which regulatory regimes apply to this engagement? *(select multiple)* Options: GLBA / Regulation P · BSA / AML / OFAC · SOX · PCI DSS · SEC / FINRA · state money transmission licensing
    3. Will the third party's outputs feed regulatory reporting or the financial statements?

    **Consumer Data and Funds** *(Financial)*

    4. Will the third party access, store or process nonpublic personal information (NPI) under GLBA? Options: over 100,000 consumers · under 100,000 · no
    5. Will the third party hold, move or have authority over customer funds? Options: with discretion · pass-through only · no
    6. Will the third party perform a consumer-facing activity subject to UDAAP or fair lending oversight?
    7. Does the third party perform or support BSA/AML, sanctions screening or KYC obligations? Options: performs on our behalf · supporting data or tooling · no
    8. Does the third party have access to core banking, payment, trading or ledger systems? Options: transactional/write · read-only · no

    **Resilience and Concentration** *(no risk domain)*

    9. What is the maximum tolerable downtime for this service? Options: under 4 hours · 4-24 hours · 1-3 days · over 3 days
    10. Does the third party rely on material subcontractors (fourth parties) to deliver the service? Options: yes, unidentified · yes, disclosed · no
    11. How readily could this third party be replaced?
    12. Where will our data be stored or processed relative to our primary regulator's jurisdiction?
    13. What is the expected spend over the life of the contract?

    Because the critical-activity answer sets a floor, a critical relationship cannot be diluted into a low rating by benign answers elsewhere, which is the failure mode an examiner looks for.

    **Typical use:** Bank and credit union vendor onboarding; critical activity designation; examiner-ready tiering; fintech partner due diligence.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Healthcare & Life Sciences Inherent Risk · 13 questions · Healthcare" icon="stethoscope">
    Leads with business associate status under [HIPAA](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-164), then works outward through data, clinical proximity and supply chain.

    **Protected Health Information** *(Compliance)*

    1. Will the third party create, receive, maintain or transmit protected health information on our behalf, making it a business associate? **Yes floors the score at 60.**
    2. How many patient or member records will the third party handle? Options: five bands from none to 100,000 or more
    3. Which categories of sensitive data are involved? *(select multiple)* Options: PHI · substance use disorder records (42 CFR Part 2) · behavioral or mental health · genetic or genomic · payment card or billing · de-identified only
    4. Will PHI be stored or processed outside the country of collection?

    **Clinical and Patient Safety Impact** *(no risk domain)*

    5. Does the service touch clinical care delivery or patient safety? Options: informs diagnosis or treatment · supports clinical workflow · administrative only
    6. Is the product a regulated medical device or software as a medical device (SaMD)? Options: FDA-regulated or CE-marked · under evaluation · no
    7. Will the third party have access to EHR or EMR systems? Options: write or integration · read-only · no
    8. Will the third party interact directly with patients or research subjects?
    9. What is the maximum tolerable downtime for this service?

    **Regulatory and Supply Chain** *(Compliance)*

    10. Which regulatory regimes apply to this engagement? *(select multiple)* Options: HIPAA / HITECH · 42 CFR Part 2 · FDA 21 CFR Part 11 · GxP (GCP, GLP, GMP) · state health privacy laws
    11. Does the third party subcontract any part of the service? Options: subcontractors handle PHI · subcontractors without PHI access · no
    12. How long would it take to replace this third party if needed?
    13. What is the expected spend over the life of the contract?

    **Typical use:** Business associate triage; digital health vendor onboarding; clinical system procurement; life sciences supplier qualification.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="AI & Emerging Tech Inherent Risk · 12 questions · AI & Emerging Tech" icon="microchip">
    For any vendor whose product embeds AI, whether or not AI is what you are buying. Aligned with the [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) and the [EU AI Act](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/).

    **AI Footprint** *(Cybersecurity)*

    1. What role does AI play in the product or service? Options: core · optional or embedded feature · none
    2. Will our data be used to train, fine-tune or otherwise improve the vendor's models? Options: across their customer base · unknown · within our tenant only · contractually prohibited
    3. What is the most sensitive data that will be sent to the model?
    4. Does the vendor disclose which foundation models and AI subprocessors it relies on? Options: not disclosed · partially · fully disclosed and contractually committed

    **Autonomy and Consequence** *(no risk domain)*

    5. What degree of autonomy does the system have? Options: consequential actions without human review · actions subject to approval · recommends only · informational output only
    6. Will the system have access to our systems, tools or credentials, for example through agentic tool use or an API integration? Options: write or transactional · read-only · no
    7. Does the system make or materially inform decisions about people, such as hiring, credit, insurance, healthcare, housing or education? **Yes floors the score at 65.**
    8. Is AI-generated output shown to customers or the public? Options: without human review · with review · internal only

    **Governance and Regulatory Exposure** *(Compliance)*

    9. Under the EU AI Act, how would this system most likely be classified? Options: prohibited or unclear · high-risk (Annex III) · limited risk with transparency obligations · minimal risk or out of scope
    10. Does the vendor maintain an AI governance program, such as documented evaluations, model cards, red-teaming or ISO/IEC 42001 certification?
    11. Could the system's output create intellectual property, defamation or copyright exposure?
    12. How critical is this AI system to the business?

    <Tip>
      Question 2 is the one that most often changes a purchase. "Unknown" is weighted almost as heavily as "yes, across the vendor's customer base" on purpose: an unanswered training question is a live exposure, not a neutral one.
    </Tip>

    **Typical use:** AI vendor onboarding; shadow AI discovery triage; EU AI Act readiness screening; agentic tooling review.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Data Privacy Inherent Risk · 10 questions · Compliance" icon="user-shield">
    Enough to decide whether a DPIA and a transfer mechanism are needed before the engagement proceeds. Aligned with the [GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj) and the major US state regimes.

    **Processing Role and Data** *(Compliance)*

    1. What role will the third party play in processing personal data? Options: joint controller · independent controller · processor on our instructions · no personal data
    2. Which categories of personal data are involved? *(select multiple)* Options: contact details and identifiers · financial or payment · location or behavioral tracking · biometric · special category (health, race, religion, union membership, sexual orientation) · children's data
    3. How many data subjects are affected? Options: five bands from under 1,000 to over 1,000,000
    4. Which privacy regimes apply to this engagement? *(select multiple)* Options: EU GDPR · UK GDPR · CCPA / CPRA · other US state privacy laws · LGPD, PIPEDA, APPI or similar

    **Transfers and Secondary Use** *(Compliance)*

    5. Will personal data be transferred outside its jurisdiction of collection? Options: no adequacy decision or safeguards · adequacy, SCCs or DPF cover · no transfer
    6. Will the third party use personal data for its own purposes, such as analytics, marketing or model training? Options: yes · aggregated or de-identified only · limited to our instructions
    7. Will the third party carry out automated decision-making or profiling? Options: with legal or similarly significant effects · profiling without significant effects · no
    8. Does the third party engage sub-processors? Options: undisclosed · disclosed with a right to object · no
    9. How long will the third party retain personal data? Options: indefinitely or at their discretion · a defined period over three years · no longer than the contract term
    10. Does the engagement involve direct marketing, cookies or cross-site tracking on our behalf?

    **Typical use:** DPIA screening; sub-processor approval; international transfer review; marketing and adtech vendor triage.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Cloud, SaaS & Hosting Provider Inherent Risk · 11 questions · Cybersecurity" icon="shield-halved">
    Inherent risk triage for SaaS applications, platform and infrastructure providers and hosting services. Covers the data placed in the service, identity and integration reach into our environment, the service model and tenancy, hosting location, availability dependence and the provider's own subprocessors. Derived from [CSA Cloud Controls Matrix (CCM) v4](https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/cloud-controls-matrix).

    **Data in the Service** *(Cybersecurity)*

    1. What is the most sensitive data that will be stored in or processed by the service? Options: Regulated data (health, payment card, government ID, financial account) · Confidential business or customer data · Internal data only · Public data only · No data is stored
    2. How many individuals' records will the service hold? Options: 1,000,000 or more · 100,000 - 999,999 · 1,000 - 99,999 · Fewer than 1,000 · None
    3. Where will our data be hosted or processed? Options: Multiple or unknown regions · Outside our home jurisdiction · Within our home jurisdiction only

    **Access and Integration** *(Cybersecurity)*

    4. How will the service connect to our environment? *(select multiple)* Options: OAuth grants or API keys with write or admin scopes · Read-only API or OAuth access · Agent or connector installed on our network · Site-to-site VPN or private network link · Directory sync or user provisioning (SCIM) · No integration
    5. Will users sign in through our single sign-on? Options: Not applicable, no user accounts · No, local accounts only · SSO available but not enforced · Yes, SSO enforced
    6. Will the provider's personnel have access to our data or tenant? Options: Yes, standing administrative access · Yes, on request for support · No, data is encrypted with keys we control

    **Service Model and Resilience** *(Financial)*

    7. Which service model best describes the offering? Options: SaaS on shared multi-tenant infrastructure · SaaS on dedicated single-tenant infrastructure · PaaS · IaaS or dedicated hosting
    8. What would an outage of the service lasting one day cause? Options: Customer-facing services or core operations stop · Significant internal disruption · Minor inconvenience
    9. Could we export our data and move to another provider within 90 days? Options: No, or the format is proprietary · Yes, with significant effort · Yes, easily
    10. Does the provider rely on subprocessors or another cloud platform to deliver the service? Options: Yes, and they can access our data · Yes, infrastructure only · No · Unknown
    11. What is the expected annual spend? Options: Over US$500,000 · Over US$100,000 up to US$500,000 · Over US$10,000 up to US$100,000 · Up to US$10,000

    **Typical use:** SaaS application onboarding; IaaS and PaaS provider selection; Hosting and colocation reviews; Shadow IT and software purchase requests.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Software Supply Chain Inherent Risk · 11 questions · Cybersecurity" icon="shield-halved">
    Inherent risk triage for suppliers whose code runs inside our environment or products: custom development, installed and on premises software, libraries and SDKs, and anything that receives automatic updates. Covers execution privilege, update channels, open source dependence, SBOM availability and code signing. Derived from [NIST SP 800-218 Secure Software Development Framework (SSDF) v1.1](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/218/final).

    **Where the Code Runs** *(Cybersecurity)*

    1. What kind of software or development work is the supplier providing? *(select multiple)* Options: Code written for us that ships in our own products · Library, SDK or component embedded in our products · Software installed on our servers, endpoints or network · Browser extension or desktop plugin · Internal tooling with no production use
    2. With what privileges will the software run? Options: Kernel, system or domain administrator level · Elevated service account · Standard user · Sandboxed or isolated **Kernel, system or domain administrator level floors the score at 60.**
    3. How many of our hosts, devices or customer deployments will run it? Options: Most or all of the fleet, or all customers · A business unit or a large subset · A handful of systems
    4. Will the software or developers handle sensitive data or secrets? Options: Yes, production data, credentials or signing keys · Yes, test or masked data only · No

    **Build and Update Channel** *(Cybersecurity)*

    5. How are updates delivered? Options: Pushed automatically by the supplier without our approval · Automatic, but we can stage or defer them · Manual, after our own testing · Manual, without our own testing · No updates are available or applied
    6. Will the supplier have access to our source code repositories or CI/CD pipelines? Options: Yes, with write or merge rights · Yes, read-only · No
    7. Are releases cryptographically signed and verifiable by us?

    **Components and Transparency** *(Compliance)*

    8. How much of the software is built from open source or third-party components? Options: Most of it · Some of it · Little or none · Unknown
    9. Will the supplier provide a software bill of materials (SBOM)? Options: No · On request · Yes, with every release
    10. Is the software end of life, or is the supplier a small team or single maintainer? Options: Yes, end of life or unsupported · Yes, small team or single maintainer · No
    11. Does the engagement require a secure software development attestation?

    **Typical use:** Installed and on premises software procurement; Outsourced or custom software development; Third-party SDKs and libraries in our products; Secure software attestation reviews.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Managed IT & Security Service Provider Inherent Risk · 10 questions · Cybersecurity" icon="shield-halved">
    Inherent risk triage for MSPs, MSSPs, MDR providers and IT outsourcers that operate inside our environment. Covers privileged and remote access, remote monitoring and management tooling, administrative rights over cloud tenants and identity, the security functions entrusted to them and how hard they are to replace. Derived from [CISA, NSA, FBI and partners: Protecting Against Cyber Threats to Managed Service Providers and their Customers (AA22-131A)](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa22-131a).

    **Privileged Access** *(Cybersecurity)*

    1. Will the provider hold administrative rights over our identity provider, directory or cloud tenants? Options: Yes, global or domain administrator · Yes, delegated or scoped admin roles · No **Yes, global or domain administrator floors the score at 70.**
    2. Which systems will the provider manage? *(select multiple)* Options: Servers and production infrastructure · End user devices · Network and firewalls · Backups and disaster recovery · Security tooling (EDR, SIEM, email security) · Business applications · No customer systems managed
    3. How will the provider access our environment remotely? Options: Persistent remote monitoring and management (RMM) agent on our hosts · Standing VPN or jump host access · Just-in-time access we approve per session · No remote access
    4. Does the provider use shared tooling or credentials across its customers? Options: Yes · Unknown · No, each customer is isolated

    **Data and Security Functions** *(Cybersecurity)*

    5. What is the most sensitive data the provider can reach? Options: Regulated or restricted data · Confidential business or customer data · Internal data only · None
    6. Will the provider be responsible for detecting or responding to security incidents? Options: Yes, 24x7 detection and response · Yes, business-hours detection and response · Yes, monitoring and alerting only · No
    7. Where are the provider's operators located? Options: Multiple countries or follow the sun · Outside our home jurisdiction · Within our home jurisdiction only
    8. Will the provider subcontract any part of the service? Options: Yes, with access to our environment · Yes, without access · No

    **Dependence** *(Financial)*

    9. If the provider stopped service tomorrow, how long could we run IT ourselves? Options: Not at all, we have no in-house capability · A few weeks · Indefinitely
    10. What is the expected annual spend? Options: Over US$500,000 · Over US$100,000 up to US$500,000 · Over US$25,000 up to US$100,000 · Up to US$25,000

    **Typical use:** Managed IT and help desk outsourcing; MSSP, MDR and SOC provider selection; Cloud tenant administration partners; Privileged remote access reviews.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Payment & Cardholder Data Inherent Risk · 10 questions · Compliance" icon="building-columns">
    Inherent risk triage for payment processors, gateways, ecommerce platforms, call centers and any third-party service provider that stores, processes or transmits cardholder data or can affect the security of our cardholder data environment. Sizes PCI DSS scope, card volume and how compliance is validated. Derived from [PCI DSS v4.0.1 Requirement 12.8: Third-Party Service Provider Management](https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/document_library/).

    **Cardholder Data Scope** *(Compliance)*

    1. Will the third party store, process or transmit cardholder data on our behalf? Options: Yes · No, but they can affect the security of our cardholder data environment · No **Yes floors the score at 60.**
    2. Which card data elements will the third party handle? *(select multiple)* Options: Sensitive authentication data (full track, CVV2, PIN) during authorization · Full primary account number (PAN) · Truncated PAN or tokens only · None
    3. How does card data reach the third party? Options: Through our systems or network before reaching them · Their script or page embedded in our website (iframe, JavaScript) · Full redirect or hosted payment page, never touching our systems · Card present terminals they supply (P2PE) · Phone or manual entry by their staff · No card data received
    4. Will the third party have access to systems in our cardholder data environment? Options: Yes, administrative or remote access · Yes, limited or monitored access · No

    **Volume and Validation** *(Compliance)*

    5. How many card transactions will the third party handle for us each year? Options: Over 6 million · 1 - 6 million · 20,000 - 999,999 · Fewer than 20,000
    6. How does the third party validate its PCI DSS compliance? Options: It does not, or validation has lapsed · Self-Assessment Questionnaire (SAQ D for service providers) · Report on Compliance (ROC) and Attestation of Compliance from a QSA · Not applicable
    7. Is the scope of their PCI DSS assessment clear about which requirements they meet for us and which remain ours? Options: No responsibility matrix exists · Partially documented · Yes, documented

    **Financial and Operational Exposure** *(Financial)*

    8. What share of our revenue flows through this third party? Options: More than 50% · 10% - 50% · Less than 10%
    9. Is there a backup payment provider we could fail over to? Options: No · Yes, with manual switchover · Yes, automatic failover
    10. Will the third party handle refunds, chargebacks or payouts on our behalf? Options: Yes, including funds settlement or payouts · Yes, refunds or chargebacks only · No

    **Typical use:** Payment processor and gateway selection; PCI DSS third-party service provider inventory (Req. 12.8); Ecommerce and checkout platform reviews; Contact center and IVR providers taking card payments.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="US Defense Supply Chain (CMMC & CUI) Inherent Risk · 12 questions · Compliance" icon="building-columns">
    Inherent risk triage for subcontractors and service providers supporting US Department of Defense contracts. Establishes whether Federal Contract Information or Controlled Unclassified Information flows to the supplier, which CMMC level the flowdown requires, export controlled data, cloud hosting of CUI, foreign ownership and DFARS 252.204-7012 incident reporting dependencies. Derived from [CMMC Program (32 CFR Part 170) and DFARS 252.204-7012](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-32/subtitle-A/chapter-I/subchapter-G/part-170).

    **Covered Information** *(Compliance)*

    1. Will the supplier process, store or transmit Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI) in performance of the subcontract? Options: Yes · Unknown, CUI marking not yet determined · No **Yes floors the score at 60.**
    2. Will the supplier receive Federal Contract Information (FCI) that is not intended for public release?
    3. Which categories of controlled data are involved? *(select multiple)* Options: Controlled technical information (CTI) · ITAR controlled technical data · EAR controlled technology (non EAR99) · Covered defense information in other CUI categories · Export control status not yet assessed · None of the above
    4. Which CMMC level does the solicitation or prime contract flow down? Options: Level 3 (DIBCAC assessment) · Level 2 (C3PAO certification assessment) · Level 2 (self-assessment) · Level 1 (self-assessment) · Not yet specified · None, no FCI or CUI involved

    **Systems and Hosting** *(Cybersecurity)*

    5. Will CUI be stored or processed in a cloud service offering? Options: Yes, in a service without FedRAMP Moderate authorization or equivalency · Yes, in a FedRAMP Moderate authorized or equivalent service · No, on premise only · No CUI involved

## How the score works

Each answer option carries a weight. The inherent risk score is the total of the weights you selected, divided by the highest total those same questions could have produced, expressed as a percentage from 0 to 100. That score then maps to an inherent risk level through the scale configured under **Configuration → Scales → Risk**.

Two things follow from that normalization:

- **Weights are relative.** They only have to be right against each other, so you can add, remove or reword questions without rebalancing the whole questionnaire.
- **Context questions are free.** Free-text, contact, country and currency questions never affect the score, so you can ask for the business owner or a service description without skewing the rating.

| Manufacturing & Physical Supplier | What would a defect in the supplier's output affect? (Safety of a finished product or end user) | 60 |
| Sanctions, Anti-Bribery & Financial Crime | Does the engagement have any connection to sanctioned countries or regions? (Third party is located in a comprehensively sanctioned jurisdiction) | 80 |
| Sanctions, Anti-Bribery & Financial Crime | Does the engagement have any connection to sanctioned countries or regions? (Goods, services or funds route through a sanctioned jurisdiction) | 70 |
| Sanctions, Anti-Bribery & Financial Crime | Is the third party subject to sanctions, including through ownership or control by sanctioned persons (for example 50% or more owned, in aggregate, by blocked persons)? (Yes) | 90 |
| ESG, Human Rights & Forced Labor | Could goods or inputs originate, even partly, in the Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region or from entities on the UFLPA Entity List? (Yes) | 75 |
| Marketing & Customer Engagement | Could the audience include children under 13 or teens? (Yes, directed at children under 13) | 60 |

When one of those answers is picked, the questionnaire cannot score below that floor no matter how the other questions are answered.

Sections are mapped to risk domains (Cybersecurity, Compliance, Financial, Reputational), so a copied template also produces per-domain inherent risk scores alongside the overall one. Sections with no domain, such as Business Impact, still count toward the overall score unless you weight your risk domains.

### Weighting risk domains

By default every scoring question counts toward the overall score on equal terms, so a domain's influence is whatever its questions add up to. To make a domain count for a fixed share instead, go to **Configuration → Risk Domains** and fill in **Weight in IRQ score** for the domains you care about. Once any domain carries a weight, the overall score becomes a weighted average of the domain scores:

- Weights are normalized by the total of the domains that scored, so they do not have to add up to 100, and an unanswered domain does not drag the score down.
- Answers in a section with no risk domain, or whose domain has no weight, no longer count. The submission's **How this score was weighted** table names how many answered questions were left out.
- A score floor still applies after the weighting.

Leave every weight empty to keep the unweighted calculation.

## Tailoring your copy

Everything is editable once you have copied a template. The adjustments worth making first:

- **Reword the spend bands** to match your own thresholds and currency. The shipped bands are US dollars and will be wrong for most workspaces.
- **Adjust weights** in the Value column on each option to reflect your risk appetite.
- **Add a score floor** on any option that should guarantee a minimum rating, your own equivalent of the critical-activity question.
- **Delete what you will not act on.** A question whose answer never changes the due diligence you run is a question that costs you response rates.
- **Wire it into intake** from **Actions → Intake Configuration** so new vendor requests are rated automatically.
- **Set risk levels** from **Actions → Configure Risk Levels** to control where the score lands.

## Use the library from MCP or the API

You can browse the library and fork a template from an MCP client, and browse it with an API key. An MCP client reads with `query_questionnaires(kind="irq_library")` and forks a template with `mutate(entity="questionnaire", action="create_from_library")`, using the slug the read returns. With an API key, `GET /v1/irq-library` lists the templates. See [List the IRQ library](/api-reference/questionnaires#list-the-irq-library).

Before an MCP client sends a write, it shows you the change and waits for you to confirm it. The [MCP tool reference](/mcp/tool-reference#questionnaires-portals-and-intake) lists every field.
