<Info>
  This guide is part of the [User Guides](/user-guides/overview) collection. It picks up where the [Contract Guardian guide](/user-guides/contract-guardian) ends: you have run a clause review, decided the flagged clauses, and sent the vendor a redline. For how those asks are written and sent, read that guide first.
</Info>

A contract review does not end when you send the redline. The vendor answers, usually weeks later, usually with a marked-up document, and you have three questions:

- **What did they change?**
- **Did they give me what I asked for?**
- **What is still open?**

A round answers all three against the review you already ran. You do not start a second review, and you do not lose the verdicts you recorded the first time.

## What a round is

One exchange: you send asks, the vendor returns a document, Coverbase reads it against what you asked for.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Round 1** | Your first clause review. You decide the flagged clauses and send a redline. |
| **Round 2** | You upload what the vendor sent back. Coverbase compares it to round 1 and tells you what moved. |
| **Round 3+** | The same again. Contracts that go three or four rounds are normal, and each one only asks about what changed. |

Your decisions carry. A clause whose language the vendor did not touch keeps the verdict you gave it, and the round says which round you gave it in. Anything the vendor edited comes back to you, whatever you decided last time, because a decision about the old language is not a decision about the new.

## Before you start

<Steps>
  <Step title="Have a completed round">
    You need a clause review on this contract with at least one ask sent to the vendor. If nothing has been sent yet, there is nothing to compare against, so run the review first.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Have the vendor's document">
    A PDF or Word file of the contract as the vendor returned it. Their cover email or response letter is useful too; you can upload both.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check your permissions">
    Starting a round needs the same contract-update permission as starting a review.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  You do not have to finish reviewing round 1 before starting round 2. If the vendor comes back early, upload their document. Clauses you had not decided yet stay undecided and carry into the new round.
</Note>

## Step 1: Upload what the vendor sent back

While a round is out with the vendor, the review page says so, and the upload sits on that same banner. **Upload vendor response** is also in the review page header, so you do not have to remember which screen you were on.

<Frame caption="A sent round, waiting on the vendor. The count is what you are waiting for.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/round-waiting-on-vendor.png" alt="Callout reading round 1 sent, 8 asks outstanding, with an upload vendor response button" />
</Frame>

Add the vendor's file on the contract's **Documents** tab, the same way you add any other contract document. That is where Coverbase asks the question that matters most here: **is this a new version of a document already on the contract, or a new document alongside it?**

- **A new version** replaces the old one. The superseded text stops being reviewed.
- **A new document** sits alongside the others, such as a cover letter or a side agreement.

Getting that wrong is the one mistake that costs you: a replacement recorded as an addition leaves both versions in scope, and the round then reports clauses from language nobody is bound by any more.

Then open **Upload vendor response** and confirm what this round reads. Everything on the contract is read unless you unpick it.

<Frame caption="Choosing what the round reads. Unpicking a document leaves it out of this round only.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/round-upload-response.png" alt="Dialog listing the contract's documents with checkboxes, a link to add the returned document, and a compare against round 1 button" />
</Frame>

Press **Compare against round 1**.

<Note>
  The round inherits the clause sets and reviewer of the round it answers. A negotiation measures the vendor's reply against what they were actually asked for, so swapping the standard mid-way would grade them on something they never saw.
</Note>

## Step 2: See what changed

The round opens on its answer, before you review a single clause.

<Frame caption="The round summary. Every bucket states what it means for you, riskiest first.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/round-what-changed.png" alt="Round summary reading the vendor answered 6 of your 9 asks, with buckets for unasked edit, countered, not addressed, accepted and unchanged" />
</Frame>

| Bucket | What it means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Unasked edit** | The vendor edited a clause you never raised, including ones you accepted in an earlier round. | Read every one. This is the bucket that catches quiet edits. |
| **Countered** | They moved, but the language still falls short of the tier you asked for. | Decide whether the counter is good enough. |
| **Not addressed** | You asked; nothing changed. | Decide whether to press again, accept the risk, or drop it. |
| **No longer present** | A clause the previous round read is not in the returned document. | Check whether it was removed deliberately. |
| **Newly present** | A clause you check for appears for the first time. | Read it; nothing about it was decided before. |
| **New standard** | You added this clause to your standard since the previous round. | Review it as you would in a first round. |
| **Accepted** | The language now meets the tier you asked for. | Nothing, unless you disagree with the read. |
| **Unchanged** | Untouched, so your earlier decision stands. | Nothing. |

**Unasked edits are listed first on purpose.** An edit in a clause you already signed off is the thing a returned document is most likely to hide, and it is the reason to read a marked-up contract with a tool rather than by eye.

**Review what needs you** opens the queue with the riskiest bucket first; **Show all** walks every clause the round read.

## Step 3: Review what needs you

The queue holds what this round needs a decision on: the clauses the vendor edited, plus the asks they left unanswered. Every row says what happened to it.

## Step 4: Decide on a counter

When you open a clause the vendor countered, the middle pane compares **what you asked for** against **what came back**, rather than making you re-read the whole clause. The changed language is highlighted, and Coverbase says in one sentence why it counts the reply as a counter rather than an acceptance.

<Frame caption="A counter. The struck words are the language you last saw; the highlighted words are what came back.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/round-reading-a-counter.png" alt="Clause comparison showing the previous round's language struck through and the vendor's returned language highlighted, above the suggested redline and an explanation of why it is a counter" />
</Frame>

Read the top block as a report and the block below it as a proposal. The top one says what the vendor did; the **Suggested redline** underneath still argues for your standard, so its struck words are the contract's and its highlighted words are yours.

A clause the vendor left alone shows the decision you already made rather than asking again:

<Frame caption="An untouched clause. The verdict you recorded in an earlier round still stands.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/round-carried-verdict.png" alt="Clause showing an unchanged badge and a line reading your round 1 decision stands, non-conforming" />
</Frame>

An ask the vendor ignored is still queued, though nothing moved, because silence is a decision you have to answer:

<Frame caption="An ask nothing came back on.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/round-not-addressed.png" alt="Clause showing a not addressed badge and the explanation that the language is unchanged" />
</Frame>

Your four verdicts are unchanged from round 1: accept as-is, flag for the vendor, accept the risk, or not applicable. **Save** records the verdict; **Skip for now** moves on without recording one.

## Step 5: Send the next round

The follow-up drafted at the end of a round carries only what is still open.

- An ask the vendor **accepted** is settled and never re-raised.
- An ask they **countered** comes back as a fresh ask about their language, not a repeat of yours.
- An ask they **did not address** is restated.
- A clause they **changed without being asked** becomes a new ask, if you flagged it.

Re-sending an ask the vendor already agreed to is how a negotiation loses credibility, so the packet will not do it.

<Note>
  Coverbase proposes which of your asks were accepted; it does not settle them quietly. When the reading is not clear-cut, the ask is left for you to confirm on the round summary, because marking an ask accepted removes it from every future message.
</Note>

## What to check on every round

<Steps>
  <Step title="Read the unasked changes first">
    Every clause in that bucket, even the ones you accepted last time.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm the new-version-versus-new-document call on the Documents tab">
    One wrong call there makes the whole round report on superseded language.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm the proposed acceptances">
    Anything Coverbase was unsure about is waiting for your tick on the summary.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Look at what was not addressed">
    Silence is an answer. Decide whether to press it again or let it go, rather than sending the same sentence a third time.
  </Step>
</Steps>
