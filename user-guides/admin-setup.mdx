Welcome. This guide takes you from a blank environment to a fully configured one: importing vendors, building the inherent risk questionnaire (IRQ), tuning scales and control sets, and configuring the templates, monitoring, and integrations everything downstream depends on.

Configuration is where the AI gets its instructions. The better this upfront work, the fewer issues your reviewers triage later — so it's worth doing thoughtfully. Most teams complete core setup in **1–2 weeks with roughly 20 hours of effort**, and your Coverbase contact runs live working sessions to do it alongside you.

<Tip>
  New to how the platform thinks? Read the two-minute [How Coverbase thinks](/user-guides/overview#how-coverbase-thinks-two-minutes-of-theory) primer first. The short version: Coverbase is **document-first** (it measures vendor evidence against your controls), **tags drive everything**, and **configuration changes apply going forward**, never retroactively.
</Tip>

## Step 1: Organization, users & branding

**What this does:** Gets your team into the platform and makes everything vendor- and stakeholder-facing carry your brand.

**How to do it:**

1. Go to **Configuration → Organization** and add your users. Assign roles as you go:
   - **Admin** — full platform access
   - **Reviewer** — assess vendors and manage workflows
   - **Requester** — submit vendor requests only
2. For a broader rollout, configure a **verified domain** so anyone from your email domain is auto-provisioned on first login — no manual account creation while you pilot.
3. Under **Branding**, upload two logos:
   - **Simple Logo** — used in the app header, navigation, and emails (recommended ~200×50px)
   - **Large Logo** — used in generated reports and vendor-facing documents, including the portal (recommended ~400×100px)

  <img src="/images/user-guides/admin-branding.png" alt="Coverbase organization and branding settings" />
</Frame>

<Tip>
  When vendors receive an assessment request, they should see **your** brand, not Coverbase's. Auto-provisioning via verified domains means your pilot team can start testing immediately.
</Tip>

## Step 2: Import your vendor portfolio

**What this does:** Loads your existing vendor inventory so assessments, monitoring, and redundancy checks have something to work against. This becomes the library you run reassessments and continuous monitoring from.

**What you'll need:** Your vendor list with attributes like name, website, description, relationship owner, and contract dates. Your Coverbase contact can provide the bulk-upload template (and an enrichment workflow that appends missing fields).

**How to do it:**

1. Navigate to **Actions → Bulk Create Vendors**.
2. Download the template to see the required fields, then map your existing data into it.
3. Upload your file.
4. Validate: confirm the vendor count matches your source file, and spot-check that field values populated correctly.

<Frame caption="Bulk Create Vendors — map your existing list into the template and import your whole portfolio at once.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/admin-vendor-import.png" alt="Bulk create vendors import screen" />
</Frame>

<Tip>
  **Start with your top 100–200 vendors** rather than your entire database. This lets you refine configuration before scaling. You can also pre-load historical documents (SOC 2s, contracts, policies) against vendor records, so your first assessments run against real evidence instead of an empty corpus.
</Tip>

## Step 3: Create your tag structure

**What this does:** Tags are the routing layer of the platform. IRQ answers apply tags; tags then decide which control sets apply, which documents are required, and which due-diligence track a vendor gets.

Design the taxonomy **before** the IRQ, because the IRQ is what populates it. A structure that works well in practice (adapt to your program):

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Criticality" icon="gauge-high">
    Standard · Important · Business Critical · Mission Critical
  </Card>
  <Card title="Data" icon="database">
    PII · PHI · Financial · Employee, plus flow/volume tags (Inbound, Outbound, Bidirectional, High/Low Volume, No Data Access)
  </Card>
  <Card title="Access" icon="key">
    Login access · PII access · On-premise · Foreign vendor
  </Card>
  <Card title="Geography" icon="earth-americas">
    US Only · EU/EEA · UK · APAC · International
  </Card>
  <Card title="Infrastructure" icon="server">
    Public Cloud · Private Cloud · Hybrid · On-Premise · Not Applicable
  </Card>
  <Card title="Size / Profile" icon="building">
    Startup through Enterprise; SaaS, Consultant, Facilities, Financial, Operations
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

**How to configure:** Go to **Configuration → Tags**, create a tag group for each category, and add the individual tags within each group.

<Frame caption="The Tags configuration screen — grouped tags for Access, Criticality, Data, Geography, Infrastructure, Size, and Vendor Profile.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/admin-tags.png" alt="Tag structure configuration grouped by category" />
</Frame>

<Note>
  Unlike a plain high/medium/low rating, tags capture the nuanced reasons **why** a vendor is risky and **how** it should be assessed. Nearly every automation you configure later keys off tags — a "Foreign" tag routes to additional controls, a "PCI" tag makes a PCI AOC required, an "AI SaaS" tag can attach a different clause set. Keep the taxonomy clean.
</Note>

## Step 4: Build and weight your IRQ

**What this does:** The inherent risk questionnaire (IRQ) is what business requesters — and Coverbase's research agent — answer at intake. Its **weights** produce the inherent risk score; its **response mapping** applies your tags. Aim for 15–25 concise questions.

**How to do it:**

1. Go to **Configuration → Questionnaires** and create your **Inherent Risk Questionnaire**. Use **Actions → Bulk Create Questions** to import an existing IRQ from the spreadsheet template rather than building question by question.
2. **Weight every answer** with a risk score (0–20 scale; higher = more risk). The sum of all maximum answer scores is the risk ceiling, and a vendor's actual answers as a percentage of that ceiling is their inherent risk score.
3. Open **Response Mapping** (the gear icon) and map answers to tags — e.g., data handled outside the US applies "Foreign"; card data applies "PCI".
4. Add **conditional logic** where follow-on questions should only appear based on a prior answer (e.g., data-flow details only if data is exchanged).
5. Set **per-question AI guidance** to control what the research agent may use when auto-answering — e.g., "only source from the vendor's trust center; exclude blogs and CVE chatter."
6. Mark judgment questions as **Required** so the AI never auto-answers them without human input.

<Frame caption="The IRQ builder — each answer carries a weight (right column), and Configure Response Mapping links answers to the tags that route the vendor.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/admin-irq-builder.png" alt="Inherent Risk Questionnaire builder with weighted answers" />
</Frame>

<Warning>
  **Weight the template before your first real intake.** The default IRQ ships with dozens of zero-value answer scores. Left unweighted, inherent risk reads artificially low across your entire portfolio — an API vendor can score like a stationery supplier.
</Warning>

### Example: mapping answers to tags

The mapping is what powers automated routing. When someone answers "This vendor will process employee data," Coverbase applies the Employee Data tag, which then triggers specific control sets and document requests.

| Question | Answer | Tags applied | Weight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What type of data will this vendor access? | Employee PII | PII Access, Employee Data, High Volume | High |
| | Public marketing data | Low Volume, No PII | Low |
| Where is this vendor located? | European Union | EU/EEA, International | Medium |
| | United States only | US Only | Low |

### Pattern: auto-escalating vendors to Critical

A common requirement: if a vendor answers "yes" to two or three specific questions, they should land in the strictest track regardless of overall score. Use two mechanisms together as redundancy:

- **Weight those questions heavily** so any "yes" pushes the score into your critical band on its own.
- **Map those answers to a tag** that routes the vendor to the stricter due-diligence track (control sets and document requirements keyed to that tag).

Scored multi-select questions with precedence logic are also supported — e.g., a data-elements question where selecting PAN automatically maxes the weight.

## Step 5: Configure risk and compliance scales

**What this does:** Defines the bands your program scores against — both the vendor-level **risk scale** and the per-control **compliance scale**.

**How to do it:**

1. Go to **Configuration → Scales → Risk Scale**. The default bands are 0–25 low, 25–50 medium, 50–75 high, 75–100 critical. Every threshold is adjustable, and you can add or remove tiers (collapse to three, lower the critical threshold, and so on).
2. Review the **compliance scale** used on controls — default is 4 (fully compliant) down to 1 (not compliant). You can rename levels, add or remove them, or go boolean.
3. **Write real criteria into each level, not just labels.** The criteria text is used when assigning scores, so "one or more failing conditions met" beats a bare "low."
4. Add **custom assessment scales** if your program rates additional dimensions (e.g., RFP alignment, ESG alignment, contractual protection).

<Frame caption="The risk scale — adjustable bands from low through critical, each with real scoring criteria.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/admin-risk-scale.png" alt="Risk scale configuration with adjustable bands" />
</Frame>

<Frame caption="The compliance scale used on individual controls — rename, add, remove, or go boolean.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/admin-compliance-scale.png" alt="Compliance scale configuration" />
</Frame>

<Accordion title="Common custom scales beyond compliance" icon="ruler">
  - **RFP alignment:** Exceeds / Meets / Partially meets / Does not meet requirements
  - **ESG alignment:** Strong / Developing / Limited / No ESG program
  - **Contractual protection:** Strong (indemnification, insurance, SLAs) / Standard / Limited / Inadequate

  Different risk domains need different criteria. Your legal team evaluating contract terms uses a different scale than your InfoSec team evaluating technical controls. Custom scales let each domain score its own way while keeping the overall picture consistent.
</Accordion>

## Step 6: Upload and tune control sets

**What this does:** Control sets are what assessments measure against. They define what "good" looks like. Coverbase ships SIG, NIST (CSF, AI RMF), ISO 27001, NYDFS, FFIEC, PCI DSS, DORA and more out of the box; your own frameworks import from a spreadsheet.

<Frame caption="The control set library — built-in frameworks plus your own, each with its controls, versions, and applicable vendors.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/admin-control-sets.png" alt="Control sets library showing NIST AI RMF and other frameworks" />
</Frame>

### Importing your own controls

1. Click **+ New Control Set → Upload Spreadsheet**, and download the template and the accompanying tips document.
2. Use an LLM to convert your existing framework (from your GRC tool, a spreadsheet, wherever it lives) into the template format — paste your controls plus the tips doc and ask for the conversion.
3. Upload **one control set at a time** and verify the import. Start with your 2–3 most critical frameworks.

### Per-set configuration

Every control has three parts — the **expectation** (what you're measuring), the **question** (how it'd be asked of a vendor if evidence is missing), and the **guidance** (how the AI should evaluate). Then, per set:

- **Applicability** — map which tags and inherent-risk levels this set applies to. This is what makes assessment assignment automatic.
- **Risk domains** — assign controls to domains (InfoSec, compliance, operational resilience) so review work can later be split across specialists.
- **Strictness** — set the evaluation toggle (lenient, standard, or strict) per set.
- **Evidence sources** — choose what the AI may draw on per control: specific document types, web search, and the vendor fact sheet. These are combinable and prioritizable (e.g., information security policy first, web search as fallback).
- **Document requirements** — mark documents required or suggested. **Required blocks portal submission until uploaded** — use it sparingly.

<Accordion title="A document-requirements pattern that works" icon="file-check">
  SOC 2 and pen test **required**; PCI AOC **required only for PCI-tagged vendors**; a bridge letter left as a **suggested** remediation when a SOC 2 is past your currency window; everything else **suggested**. Default to suggested unless it's a hard gate.
</Accordion>

### Per-control tuning

Open any control to edit its expectation, weight, and guidance. **Guidance is where conditional logic and exceptions live** — "mark fully compliant if the vendor is not a professional services company with a physical on-site presence," or "accept an infosec training attestation as sufficient evidence."

<Tip>
  You don't have to write guidance up front. Reviewers refine it from real results using **Correct the AI** (see the [Analyst & Reviewer guide](/user-guides/analyst-reviewer#step-4-correct-the-ai)), and every accepted correction applies to all future assessments. **Specific guidance beats cranking the global strictness toggle** — it's more effective and more defensible to a regulator.
</Tip>

<Note>
  **Framework gaps are normal.** Not every framework covers every topic — NIST CSF, for example, doesn't explicitly cover penetration testing. When your program cares about something a framework doesn't name, add a control for it and point the guidance at the specific evidence.
</Note>

## Step 7: Assessment plans and reassessment cadence

**What this does:** Decides which assessment a vendor gets, and when they get looked at again.

**How to do it:**

- Build **assessment plans** that bundle control sets by tier (e.g., high/critical vendors get the fuller plan). Plans can be assigned statically or dynamically off tags and inherent risk.
- Set the **reassessment cadence** in assessment settings. It defaults off residual risk (e.g., low = every 3 years) but can key off inherent risk instead — and it's fully overridable per vendor.
- Layer on **trigger-based reassessments** so a Radar event or a risk-score change can open an off-cycle review automatically.

  <img src="/images/user-guides/admin-assessment-plans.png" alt="Assessment plans configuration" />

<Frame caption="Reassessment cadence — a default per risk tier, fully overridable per vendor, with trigger-based off-cycle reviews.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/admin-reassessment.png" alt="Reassessment cadence settings" />

<Accordion title="A sensible starting cadence" icon="calendar-days">
  - **Critical:** every 12 months
  - **High:** every 24 months
  - **Medium:** every 36 months
  - **Low:** manual only

  Manual reassessment tracking fails at scale — spreadsheets go stale and vendors slip through. Automated schedules keep consistent oversight without the admin overhead.
</Accordion>

## Step 8: Report and email templates

**What this does:** Makes every output — assessment readouts and vendor communications — land in your organization's format and voice.

**Report templates.** Go to **Configuration → Assessment Settings → Assessment Report Templates**. Build a branded template using **tokens** (fields from the assessment and vendor record) plus **custom AI prompts** for freeform sections — e.g., "give a meaningful analysis of the limitation of liability as it relates to their DPA." No house format yet? Start from the Coverbase best-practice template with your branding.

<Frame caption="The report template builder — tokens pull structured fields, and custom AI prompts fill freeform sections in your voice.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/admin-report-template.png" alt="Assessment report template builder" />
</Frame>

<Note>
  Three exports come built in: **Standard PDF**, **Executive Summary**, and **Full** (every control with all evidence — the one examiners enjoy).
</Note>

**Email templates.** Configure vendor email templates for portal invitations and follow-ups: sender identity, instructions, reminder cadence (e.g., every 2 days), and whether the internal requester is auto-CC'd. Attach a **pre-signed MNDA** to invitations so legal back-and-forth doesn't stall document sharing.

## Step 9: Configure Radar (continuous monitoring)

**What this does:** Sets up the external intelligence layer. **Sources** feed **detectors**, and detectors raise **alerts** on your third and fourth parties.

**How to do it:**

1. Review the out-of-the-box **sources**: SEC filings, CVE feeds, CISA advisories, security and financial news — pulled several times daily. Add any public RSS feed at no extra cost, or connect a ratings platform (e.g., Black Kite) as an API source.
2. Create **detectors** around what you actually act on — breaches and vulnerabilities, supply-chain disruption, SEC/regulatory actions. Each detector takes plain-language guidance, example events to hone in on, impacted risk domains, and a severity threshold.
3. **Test each detector before enabling**, and start with tight severity thresholds.

<Frame caption="Radar detectors — each one is scoped to what you'll act on, with categories, reviewers, and an enable toggle.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/admin-radar-detectors.png" alt="Radar detector configuration list" />
</Frame>

<Frame caption="Radar sources — built-in feeds plus any public RSS feed or connected ratings API.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/admin-radar-sources.png" alt="Radar sources configuration" />
</Frame>

<Warning>
  **Tuning is the whole game.** The failure mode of continuous monitoring is noise — a single well-covered vendor can generate a dozen hits in a week. Keep detectors narrow and severities high at first, review what fires for two weeks, then loosen deliberately. Fourth-party relationships (extracted automatically from SOC 2s, subprocessor lists, and SBOMs) mean one event can implicate many vendors — which is exactly why precision matters.
</Warning>

## Step 10: Enable obligations extraction

**What this does:** Turns on extraction of what vendors require of **you** — CUECs from SOC reports (verbatim) and shared responsibilities from contracts, DPAs, and terms.

**How to do it:**

1. In settings, enable **auto-create** for CUECs and obligations from uploaded documents, and confirm which document types trigger extraction (SOC 1 Type 2, SOC 2 Type 2, contracts).
2. **Re-trigger analysis** on documents uploaded before you enabled this — extraction only runs on documents processed after the setting is on.
3. Optionally upload your internal control set so extracted obligations can be auto-checked against controls you already have in place.

## Step 11: Integrations and Export API (optional)

**What this does:** Exposes Coverbase data to external systems (GRC platforms, ticketing, data warehouses).

**How to do it:**

1. Navigate to **Organization → API Keys → Add new key**; name it, set an expiration, and store the bearer token securely.
2. Go to **Configuration → Export API** and map which Coverbase objects and fields to expose; note the source schema and GET endpoint shown on screen.
3. For GRC integrations (e.g., findings out to your audit platform, controls back in), scope what needs to flow — findings, residual-risk ratings, control-environment scores — with your Coverbase contact.

<Frame caption="The Export API configuration — map objects and fields, then read the source schema and GET endpoint right on screen.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/admin-export-api.png" alt="Export API configuration" />
</Frame>

<Note>
  Coverbase uses a **pull model** — external systems request data via the API; there's no automatic push. Vendor status pages can also be connected for SLA-adjacent monitoring. For the full developer reference, see the [Export API concepts](/export-api-concepts).
</Note>

## Admin quick reference

Five rules that save the most grief:

<CardGroup cols={1}>
  <Card title="Changes aren't retroactive" icon="clock-rotate-left">
    IRQ and control-set edits apply going forward; use a bulk reassessment to apply new standards to existing vendors. Control sets are version-controlled — edits create a new version.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Weight before you launch" icon="scale-unbalanced">
    Zero-value IRQ scores are the most common cause of "why is everything low risk?"
  </Card>
  <Card title="Tags drive everything" icon="tags">
    Control-set applicability, document requirements, clause sets, and escalation tracks all key off tags — keep the taxonomy clean.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Required means blocking" icon="lock">
    A required document stops portal submission entirely. Default to suggested unless it's a hard gate like a PCI AOC.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Guidance beats strictness" icon="wand-magic-sparkles">
    Specific control guidance (who's exempt, what evidence counts) is more defensible to a regulator — and more effective — than cranking the global strictness toggle.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Frequently asked questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="How long does initial setup take?">
    Most organizations complete core configuration in 1–2 weeks with roughly 20 hours of effort. Organizations with complex control sets or extensive integrations may need 4–6 weeks.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Do we need to configure all control frameworks at once?">
    No. Start with your 2–3 most critical frameworks (typically NIST CSF and one industry-specific standard). Add more as your program matures.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What if we don't have an existing IRQ?">
    Use Coverbase's enhanced template. It includes industry best practices and can be customized to your requirements.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How does AI assessment actually work?">
    When you initiate an assessment, Coverbase searches the vendor's website, trust center, and security documentation; analyzes uploaded documents (SOC 2 reports, contracts, policies); pulls data from integrated services; evaluates all evidence against your control requirements; and surfaces gaps as issues for human review — generating follow-up questions only for what it couldn't verify.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What if the AI makes a mistake?">
    Coverbase assumes AI will make errors. Every assessment includes a human review phase where your team validates findings before finalizing, and every result carries citations, making errors easy to spot and correct. Think of it as AI drafting ~90% of the assessment, with your team quality-checking the rest.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
