The contracts module has two screens: the list page, where you see the whole portfolio at once, and the contract record, where you work one agreement. This guide covers what each part shows and what you can change.

## The contracts list page

<Frame caption="The contracts list page: the attention cards, the toolbar, and the contract table.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/contracts-portfolio.png" alt="Contracts list page with attention cards, toolbar, and contract table" />
</Frame>

Two sections stack down the page: the attention cards, then the list. The list has two views, a table and a renewal runway, and a toggle on the toolbar switches between them.

Beside the page title are three links: **Configuration** for lifecycle stages and reminders, **Guides** for this documentation, and **Reports** for the dashboards built on the portfolio.

### Attention cards

<Frame caption="The four attention cards. The line under each figure is the part that cannot wait.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/contracts-key-statistics.png" alt="Attention cards showing notice deadlines, renewal decisions needed, missing renewal details, and reviews needed" />
</Frame>

Four cards sit in one row. Each is a queue that needs someone to act, and the line under the figure says how urgent it is.

| Card | What it counts | The line beneath it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Notice deadlines** | Contracts whose notice date is overdue or falls inside the next 90 days. | The window, and how many are already overdue. |
| **Renewal decisions needed** | Contracts inside that window with no renew or terminate decision recorded for this term. | How many are still ahead and fall due in the next 30 days. |
| **Missing renewal details** | Contracts set to auto-renew with no renewal term recorded. | How many of those terms end in the next 90 days. |
| **Reviews needed** | Contracts with a clause awaiting a decision, an assignment clause to re-read after a change of control, or a recorded value that disagrees with the document. | What the card covers. |

Click a card to filter the list below to exactly those contracts. Click it again to clear the filter. Each card is a saved view, so the filter it applies can be edited like any other, and the three review queues folded into **Reviews needed** are still available on their own from the view selector.

<Note>
  **Reviews needed counts contracts, not problems.** A contract with an open clause and a value conflict is one review, because the list the card opens can only show it once.
</Note>

### The toolbar

Under the cards, one row holds the controls for the list: **Search**, the view selector (**All contracts** and your saved views), **Filter**, display options for the table's columns, the toggle between the **table** and the **renewal runway**, and **Preview**. On the right, **Actions** opens exports, notifications, uploads, bulk create, and **Start a review** for a clause review, and **New contract** creates one.

The view toggle and **Preview** remember your choice in this browser. They do not change anything for your colleagues.

### The contract table

The table opens on the renewal story, left to right: **Contract**, **Vendor**, **Status**, **Notice due**, **Renewal decision**, **Renewal**, **Renewal term**, **Current term end**, **Relationship owner**, **Contract value**, and **Updated**. Columns marked **AI** hold values Coverbase extracted from the documents rather than values someone typed. The column picker offers the rest: **Term start**, **Effective date**, **Initial term**, **Termination notice**, **Contract length**, **Action date**, **Renewals applied**, **Contract type**, **Committed amount**, and every contract component.

A value nobody has recorded reads **Not recorded**. It turns orange where the gap is yours to close: a contract that renews automatically with no **Renewal term** will roll forward on a term nobody wrote down. A contract renewed by agreement has no renewal term, so that cell reads **Not applicable**.

Every row ends in a menu with the same four actions:

| Action | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Preview** | Turns on preview mode and shows this contract beside the list. |
| **View details** | Opens the contract record. |
| **Start clause review** | Starts a Contract Guardian review on this contract. |
| **Archive** | Hides the contract from the list after you confirm. |

### Previewing contracts

<Frame caption="Preview mode. The selected contract is highlighted in the list and shown beside it.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/contracts-preview.png" alt="Contract list with one row highlighted and a panel on the right showing the vendor, the contract name, its summary, and its renewal terms" />
</Frame>

Click **Preview** in the toolbar to look through contracts without leaving the list. A panel opens beside the table, or beside the runway, and shows the first contract. While preview mode is on:

- Click any row to show it in the panel. You do not need to close the panel first, and the rest of the page stays usable.
- The contract you are previewing stays highlighted in the list.
- Use the up and down arrows at the top of the panel, or the up and down arrow keys on your keyboard, to move to the previous or next contract. They follow the list as you have filtered and sorted it, and continue onto the next page. The panel shows where you are, for example **2 of 230**.
- Click **Open contract** at the bottom of the panel to open the full record.

The panel shows the vendor first. If the contract is an order form, SOW, or amendment under another agreement, that agreement comes next, and you can click it to open it. Then come the contract name, type, and status. Below that are the details that do not fit in a column: the summary or description, the term and renewal dates, the contract value and payment terms, owners, services, documents, and notes.

On a narrower screen, the panel opens over the right side of the list instead of beside it. The rows it does not cover still work the same way.

To leave preview mode, click **Preview** again or close the panel. With preview mode off, clicking a row opens the contract as usual. Holding Cmd or Ctrl while you click opens it in a new tab in either mode.

### The renewal runway

<Frame caption="The renewal runway. Each row is a contract, drawn on a year of days from today.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/contracts-renewal-runway.png" alt="Renewal runway with one lane per contract showing the notice deadline, the required notice span, and the current term end" />
</Frame>

Switch the toggle to the runway and the same rows are drawn as a calendar: one lane per contract, on a year of days from today. Filters, search, sorting, and paging carry over from the table.

Each lane carries three things, named in the legend:

| Mark | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Hollow circle | **Notice due**: the last day you can still give notice. |
| Line | **Required notice**: the span between the notice deadline and the term end. |
| Filled dot | **Current term end**: when the term ends. |

Today is the circled day at the left, with a line down every lane. Hover a mark to read its date, and click a row to open the contract. The row menu is the same one the table has.

A lane with no notice date says **Notice due date not recorded** where the mark would be, with a dotted line up to the term end. A notice date that has already passed sits on today as a filled mark, and its hover gives the date it fell on. A term end more than a year out runs off the right edge.

<Tip>
  The notice deadline is the last day you can act, and it always comes before the term end. Read the lanes by their hollow circles first: that is the column of dates you are working to.
</Tip>

<Tip>
  To find the contracts that cannot renew, filter on **Auto Renewal** is `automatic` and **Renewal Term** is empty. Those are the ones that will be marked expired instead of rolling forward.
</Tip>

## The contract record

<Frame caption="A contract record. Facts on the left, relationships and dates on the right.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/contracts-detail-overview.png" alt="Contract record showing details, summary, components, insights, and the right rail" />
</Frame>

Open a contract from the table, the runway, or a search result. The **Overview** tab is laid out in two columns.

The main column holds, in order:

1. **Details** and **Properties**, the contract's own facts
2. **Commercial terms**, the term, renewal, notice, cost, and legal summary in one form
3. **Summary**, the AI read of the agreement
4. The document block: key documents, document family, contract components, and document insights
5. **Reviews**

The right rail holds:

1. **Vendor**
2. **Lifecycle**
3. **Contract timeline**
4. **Notifications**
5. **Renewal notes**
6. **Team**

### The details card

The details card shows one row per field that has an answer. A field the contract does not carry is not drawn, so the card stays short on a thin record and grows as extraction fills it in.

Eight fields are available: description, contract value, payment terms, services, term start date, effective date, term end date, and auto renewal.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add a field">
    Click the **+** in the card header and pick the field. It appears as an empty row you can type into. Only fields not already shown are offered.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Remove a field from view">
    Hover the row and click the **X**. The row disappears from this contract's card.
  </Step>
  <Step title="See the yearly breakdown">
    Hover **Contract value**. An option to add a year-by-year breakdown appears next to it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Removing a field hides it on that one contract, in your browser. It does not delete the value, does not affect other contracts, and does not change what anyone else sees. Add it back from the **+** menu at any time.
</Note>

### Saving, and undoing a save

The editable rows on a contract commit when you click away. There is no **Save** button, so the confirmation is the toast that appears in the corner: "Description saved", "Renewal term saved", and so on.

Every confirmation carries **Undo**. Click it and the field goes back to the value it held before that save.

<Note>
  **Undo is offered for one save at a time.** Once you edit the same field again, the earlier offer disappears, because putting back a value from two edits ago would throw away the edit in between. Finish an edit, check the confirmation, then move on.
</Note>

If a save fails, the toast says so and names the field. The value stays in the box, so you can click away again to retry rather than retyping it.

### The commercial terms card

<Frame caption="Commercial terms: the term, what it renews into, the notice deadline, the renewal decision, the cost, and the legal summary.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/contract-commercial-terms.png" alt="Commercial terms card showing term and renewal, renewal decision, cost, and legal summary" />
</Frame>

These are the questions people ask together, so they sit in one card rather than across the timeline rail, the details card, and the value card. Four sections stack down it.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Term and renewal">
    **Initial term** is how long the first term runs. **Renewal term** is what each renewal adds. **Auto-renews** is whether renewal happens by itself, needs a manual step, or does not happen. **Notice period (days)** is how much warning the vendor is owed.

    Below them, read-only, are the two dates those values produce: **Notice due to vendor** and **Current term ends**.

    A badge in the card header shows which term you are on. **Term 4** means the contract has renewed three times.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Renewal decision">
    Four buttons: **Not decided**, **Renew**, **Terminate**, **Under review**. The current decision is the filled one.

    Under them is a free-text note for why, and who you asked. Both are visible on the renewal runway and filterable across the portfolio, so "which renewals has nobody decided yet" is a question you can answer without opening records one at a time.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Cost">
    The contract value and its currency, plus the year-by-year breakdown when the spend is not flat across the term.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Legal summary">
    The AI read of the agreement, shown here so you do not have to scroll to it while you are confirming the terms above. It is read-only in this card. Edit or regenerate it from the **Summary** card below.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Warning>
  **A contract set to auto-renew with no renewal term cannot renew.** Renewal needs both: the setting that says it renews, and a term to renew into. With only the first, the contract is marked expired on the term end its paperwork names, which can be years in the past.

  The card says so at the top when it applies, and filling in **Renewal term** clears it. The next nightly run then renews the contract and puts its status back to active.
</Warning>

<Frame caption="The warning shown on a contract set to auto-renew with no renewal term recorded.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/contract-renewal-term-missing.png" alt="Commercial terms card showing the missing renewal term warning above the term fields" />
</Frame>

<Note>
  If a contract renews while you are recording a decision about the term that just ended, the save is refused and the card tells you the term moved. That is deliberate: the decision was about the old term, and the new one deserves a fresh look. Read the new term, then record the decision again.
</Note>

### The vendor card

<Frame caption="The vendor card. Who the counterparty is, what else they are paid, and how they were rated.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/contract-vendor-context.png" alt="Vendor card showing other contracts, status, owners, risk tier, and assessment dates" />
</Frame>

The top of the right rail answers the questions that decide how carefully the rest of the page should be read. The same 90 day notice period is routine on a design tool and a board-level problem on the vendor holding your customer data.

| Row | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| **Other contracts** | How many other live agreements you have with this vendor, and what they are worth. Click through to see them. |
| **Relationship owners** and **Risk analysts** | Who to ask. |
| **Risk tier** | The vendor's inherent risk rating. |
| **Last assessment** and **Next assessment** | When you last looked at them, and when you look again. |

Click the vendor name to open the full vendor record.

<Note>
  **Unavailable** in the risk tier or last assessment row means the lookup failed, not that the vendor is unrated or never assessed. Reload the page. If it keeps saying **Unavailable**, the vendor record itself will tell you.
</Note>

### The documents tab

<Frame caption="The Documents tab: the document table, then key documents, the family tree, components, and insights.">
  <img src="/images/user-guides/contracts-detail-documents.png" alt="Documents tab showing the document table above the document summary block" />
</Frame>

The **Documents** tab opens on the document table. Below it sits the same document block you see on the Overview tab: key documents beside the document family, then contract components, then document insights.

Both tabs render the same block, so the two views cannot disagree about the same files.
