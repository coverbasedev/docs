Each vendor with a completed assessment has a monitoring record: everything monitoring observed between that assessment and the next. Coverbase reads the whole record and resolves it to a **relationship outlook**, a tiered view of the relationship that changes as the evidence does. It does not turn a security rating into an incident probability. Every outlook names the factors behind it, so you can see why it reads the way it does and what to test next.

The mistake people make most often is reading Stable as clean. Check the confidence beside it first: a Stable outlook at Low confidence rests on thin evidence and earns no scope credit.

## The five outlooks

| Outlook | When |
| --- | --- |
| **Stable** | Monitoring covered enough of the window, nothing material is adverse, and the evidence matches the vendor's baseline. |
| **Watch** | One or more developing, isolated or uncertain conditions warrant closer attention but do not show material deterioration. |
| **Elevated** | Persistent deterioration, two or more corroborating signals, or a material unresolved condition. |
| **Critical** | A confirmed threat, a critical event or incident, or a severe unresolved condition such as a critical finding past due. |
| **Insufficient data** | Monitoring covered too little of the window to rate the relationship, and nothing on the record is adverse. |

Adverse evidence outranks thin coverage: a disclosed incident on a vendor you barely monitored still reads Critical, not Insufficient data.

The outlook is computed by rule from the record, never by a model, each time the record syncs: when a signal, finding or vendor change arrives, and every night.

## Confidence and trend

Two qualifiers sit beside the outlook and never change it.

- **Confidence** (High, Moderate or Low) says how much weight the outlook can bear. It starts from how much of the window monitoring covered, then drops a step when no outside-in rating is reporting, when the external scan's own coverage is thin, or when the window has been open less than 30 days.
- **Trend** (Improving, Unchanged or Worsening) compares the outlook with 30 days ago. When the tier has not moved, a rating that fell or rose past its Watch threshold in the last 30 days, or a new adverse event, sets the direction.

A vendor's card might read **Stable · High confidence · Unchanged**, with the sentence "97% monitoring coverage, no material adverse events, no high-severity open findings, and a stable external security posture."

## What moves the outlook

| Evidence | How it counts |
| --- | --- |
| Radar alerts, cases and manual events | A critical event or a confirmed threat reads Critical. A Radar case or a disclosed high-severity incident reads Elevated. A high-severity alert reads Watch, and Elevated once it has stayed unresolved for 30 days. Anything smaller reads Watch. A waived entry never counts. |
| Findings | Weighed by severity, age, recurrence, remediation and relevance, not counted. A critical finding past due reads Critical. A high finding past due, or a medium one past due in privileged access or vulnerability management, reads Elevated. An open high finding not yet due reads Watch, as do findings that recur in the same risk domain. Low findings remediated on schedule do not move it. |
| Outside-in ratings | Coverbase Security Posture and licensed ratings such as Bitsight. A fall past the Watch threshold reads Watch; a fall past the Elevated threshold that holds for 30 days reads Elevated. A thin or mostly indirect scan never raises the outlook past Watch. A ransomware-exploited vulnerability on the vendor's estate reads Elevated. |
| Corroboration | Two independent signal families (Radar, findings, external posture) adverse at once read Elevated. |
| Persistence | A Watch that holds for 60 days reads Elevated. |
| Screening, financial health, Zero Touch and relationship changes | A confirmed screening match reads Elevated; the others read Watch. |

The vendor also gets credit. **In the vendor's favor** lists sufficient coverage, a stable or improving rating, no material incidents, few open findings and none material, findings closed on time, no change to the service or data access, and how long the outlook has held Stable.

## When outside-in ratings do not apply

An outside-in rating describes the vendor's observable estate. When your organization runs the infrastructure behind the service, or the rated estate is not what your relationship depends on, turn off **External posture applies** on the vendor's monitoring record. The ratings stay on the record but stop moving the outlook, and the choice carries to the vendor's next record.

If you already keep these vendors in a Bitsight tier, such as **Alternative Approach**, name the tier under **Alternative Approach tiers in Bitsight** instead of switching each record. Every vendor Bitsight files under that tier has its ratings set aside, and the record's **Confidence** card names the tier. Moving a vendor out of the tier in Bitsight brings its ratings back on the next sync. Coverbase reads the tier on every Bitsight pull. When a Bitsight company's **Custom ID** matches a vendor's **External ID**, that match comes first, before the company's domain or name.

## Rating floors by inherent risk

A rating can also be judged against a floor for the vendor's inherent risk tier, in the rating's own units: for example a Bitsight floor of 740 for Critical vendors, 700 for High and 640 for Medium. A rating below its tier's floor adds a Watch condition to the outlook, and a Watch held for 60 days reads Elevated. A rating never acts on its own: the outlook does. A tier can also stop monitoring ratings altogether, in which case they stay on the record but do not move the outlook.

## Responding to a worsened outlook

When an outlook worsens into Watch, Elevated or Critical, the response rules decide what happens, by the vendor's inherent risk tier:

- **Notify POCs**: tell the vendor's points of contact, chosen under **Points of contact** (below).
- **Create finding**: open one finding on the vendor, which moves into your finding remediation process.
- **Targeted inquiry**: raise a task for the risk analyst to send the vendor a specific questionnaire or request for information about the condition, not a full assessment.
- **Escalate internally**: raise a task for an internal review of what the condition warrants, which can lead to an out-of-cycle assessment.

**Points of contact** sets who **Notify POCs** reaches: risk analysts, relationship owners, the owners of the vendor's services, engagements or contracts, and any person field on the vendor or its services, such as a PVM or Risk Office POC. A user group reaches its members, and each person is told once. The response on the record counts them by role, for example "1 risk analyst, 2 relationship owners and 2 people in PVM POC".

A Low inherent risk vendor turning Elevated need not get the same response as a Critical one. Each rule acts only at or above the confidence you set (Moderate by default). Improvements and a record's first reading never act.

The rules run in one of three modes. **Preview**, the default, records what each rule would have done, on the record and on **Radar → Outlook**, without notifying anyone or creating anything. Run Preview against your portfolio to see how much the rules would generate, tune them, then switch to **Live**. **Off** turns them off.

## What it means for the next assessment

At reassessment, the record's history and confidence choose the type of assessment:

| Type | When |
| --- | --- |
| Zero Touch | Stable for the credit period, High confidence |
| Lighter Touch | Stable for the credit period, Moderate confidence: focus on your key controls |
| Risk-Focused | Watch or Elevated in one or two areas, Moderate confidence or above, and nothing outside them: test those areas |
| Full assessment | Insufficient data, Low confidence, a broad concern, or Stable not yet held long enough |
| Out of cycle | Critical |

The credit period is 90 days by default. A Stable outlook earns it only with coverage at the policy minimum and no material or overdue open findings. The areas a Risk-Focused assessment tests are the risk domains of the findings driving the outlook, the risk domains of the Radar detectors behind its alerts, and external security posture when a rating drives it. Map a detector to the risk domains it speaks to (your SIG domains, for example) under the detector's **Risk domains**, and its alerts count toward those areas. An alert from a detector mapped to no domain, a disclosed incident or a screening match calls for a full assessment. The scoping task and the assessment's **Scoping Basis** list the focus areas.

When an outlook turns **Critical**, Coverbase raises a scoping task for the vendor that day, due in two days, and the button on the record reads **Start out-of-cycle assessment**. Creating the assessment from the record cites it: the outlook, its confidence and trend, the assessment type and the factors are stored on the assessment, so the reasoning survives after the record moves on.

## The portfolio view

**Radar → Outlook** shows every open relationship by outlook: how many sit in each tier now and how that changed over 30 days, which relationships changed outlook and why, the types of the next assessments, what the response rules did (or would have done in Preview) over 30 days by action and inherent risk tier, and a list you can filter by tier, sorted most concerning first. Select a tier to filter the list.

To chart outlooks on a dashboard, slice a monitoring record chart by **Outlook**, **Outlook Confidence** or **Outlook Trend**.

## Set it up

Under **Configuration → Assessment Settings → Reassessments → Monitoring**:

1. Under **What Counts Toward the Outlook**, choose which sources count and their severity floors.
2. Under **Coverage**, set the minimum monitored share for a reliable outlook (80% by default).
3. Under **External Security Posture**, set for each rating the fall that reads Watch and the fall that reads Elevated, in the rating's own units, or turn a rating off.
4. Under **Response by Inherent Risk**, choose the actions for each tier and outlook, the mode (Preview, Live or Off), the confidence the rules need, the **Points of contact** Notify reaches, and who escalations go to.
5. Under **Rating Floors by Inherent Risk**, set each rating's floor per tier, or turn rating monitoring off for a tier, and name your **Alternative Approach tiers in Bitsight**.
6. Under **Assessment Type by Monitoring History**, choose the plan, and whether to launch a Zero Touch run, for each type, and set **Days Stable before scope credit**.
7. Keep **Hold automatic creation unless the outlook is Stable** on to make the scheduled job wait for someone to scope every other outlook.

To be told when an outlook changes, keep the **Relationship outlook changed** notification on. It goes to the vendor's risk analysts. The **Relationship outlook turned Elevated or Critical** workflow template raises a review task the day it happens.

## Troubleshooting

| What you see | What is happening |
| --- | --- |
| The outlook reads **Pending**. | The record has not synced since monitoring records were turned on. The nightly sweep computes it for every open record, or a new signal, finding or vendor change does it sooner. |
| The outlook reads **Insufficient data** for a vendor you know well. | Monitoring covered less of the window than the policy minimum under **Coverage**, and nothing on the record is adverse. Check which sources watch the vendor. |
| The outlook is Stable but the next assessment is a full one. | Scope credit needs the outlook to hold Stable for the credit period, confidence above Low, coverage at the minimum, and no material or overdue open findings. The factors and confidence reasons on the record show which one is not met. |
| A rating fell and the outlook did not move. | The fall is smaller than the rating's Watch threshold, the rating is turned off under **External Security Posture**, **External posture applies** is off on the vendor's record, or Bitsight files the vendor under one of your **Alternative Approach tiers**. |
| A large fall in Coverbase Security Posture reads only Watch. | The scan's own coverage is thin or mostly indirect, so it cannot raise the outlook past Watch. |
| The button on the record reads **Start out-of-cycle assessment**. | The outlook is Critical. Coverbase has also raised a scoping task for the vendor, due in two days. |
| An outlook worsened and nothing was created. | The rules are in Preview or Off, the tier has no action for that outlook, or the outlook was read with less confidence than the rules need. The record's Response panel says which. |
| A response shows **Not taken**. | Nobody could receive it: no one holds the roles under **Points of contact** for the vendor, or no escalation assignee is set. |
| A vendor with a finding in one domain gets a full assessment. | Something else drives the outlook too, such as an alert from a detector mapped to no risk domain. Map the detector to its domains, or test the vendor in full. |
| A vendor has no **Relationship outlook** card. | The vendor has no completed assessment yet, so there is no window to monitor, or monitoring records are not turned on for your organization. |
